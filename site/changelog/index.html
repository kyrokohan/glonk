Unreleased

Added

  • Camera.LookAt(target) turns a camera to look at a place from where it is: it sets Yaw and Pitch so that Look points from Position to the target and Project puts the target in the middle of the frame, for a camera that follows a thing, watches a door or circles a model. A program worked the two angles out itself, which rests on what only mesh knows: that a yaw of 0 looks down +z, which way a positive yaw and pitch turn, and that Look turns by the pitch before the yaw, so a wrong sign looked at the back of the scene (#136). The pitch is at most a quarter turn, a target straight above or below keeps the yaw the camera had, and a target at the camera's own position, or one that is not a finite number, leaves the camera as it was. It is the first method of Camera that changes it, and so the first that takes a pointer.

0.13.0 - 2026-11-20

On top of 0.12.0 this release compiles for the browser and adds small drawing tools: filled rectangles of cells, drawing over a scene, every ray of the screen at once, letters in a frame's picture, borders that join, and discs with a soft edge. Nothing was removed or renamed and no signature changed, but ImageOptions gained a field; what draws differently is under Changed and Fixed. The module needs the same versions of golang.org/x/sys and golang.org/x/term as 0.12.0.

Added

  • glonk compiles for the browser, GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm, and CI checks that it still does. A build for js could not compile before, because the list of signals that end Run named SIGHUP, which Go's js port does not define; there the list leaves it out, and native systems keep the same three signals. A browser has no terminal, so Run there runs headless only: with Config.Input, Output, Width and Height set, and a page of its own that passes the keys in and draws the frames.
  • FrameBuffer.FillRect(x, y, w, h, cell) fills a rectangle of cells, as Canvas.FillRect fills pixels: the background under a popup, a cleared part of the screen, a band behind a status line, the filled part of a gauge, each of which was a loop of Set. It is cut to the frame, a width or height that is not positive draws nothing, no position or size can overflow, and like Set it never reads or writes depth. It allocates nothing.
  • Canvas.ClearDepth() and FrameBuffer.ClearDepth() set every depth back to infinitely far and keep what is drawn, to draw over a scene: a tool in the player's hand or a model in a menu, with a camera of its own, lands on top of the scene while its own faces still hide each other. Clear empties the pixels and the depth together, so a program had to compose the scene, clear the canvas and compose again with ComposeRect, which loses Seamless halves, or shrink the mesh up against its camera. FrameBuffer.ClearDepth resets the canvas's depth too, as Clear empties both. The pixels drawn before then compose with an infinite depth, so cells drawn with a depth after Compose land on top of them.
  • Camera.Rays(w, h) and Camera.RaysPixels(canvas) give every ray of a grid of cells or of a canvas's pixels at once, as a mesh.Rays: the ray through the middle of cell or pixel (i, j) starts at Origin and goes along First + i*Across + j*Down, the direction Ray or RayPixels gives for it, to within rounding. A program that draws a whole canvas by rays, a ray marcher, a sky by direction, a height map, called RayPixels for every pixel, which works out the camera's angles every time; a camera's ray is linear in the place on the screen, so three vectors give them all. Across and Down have nothing along Look, so every ray stays one deep and t along it is the depth ProjectPixels gives. ok is false where Ray's would be: a camera that sees nothing, a grid with no height, a position, yaw or pitch that is not a number, and a nil canvas. On a canvas of 240 by 134 pixels it takes 0.14 ms to step through every ray, where RayPixels for each pixel takes 2 ms (BenchmarkRaysPixels, BenchmarkRayPixelsEachPixel), and nothing is allocated.
  • ImageOptions.Text draws the letters of a frame in its picture: with it, FrameBuffer.Image draws each printable ASCII character as a letter of the 5 by 7 font that Canvas.DrawText uses, in its foreground over its background, where it drew a mark, a rectangle that showed where text was and in what color but not what it said. A screenshot for a README or a bug report, or a stored picture checked by eye, showed every menu, label and number as bars. A letter is drawn at the largest whole size that leaves a blank column beside it, Scale/6, so letters appear from a Scale of 6, and it sits in the middle of the cell, rounded up and to the left. A wide or control character is drawn as ?, as the terminal is sent one, and box lines, shades and every other character outside ASCII stay marks. It is off by default, and at the usual Scale of 4 there is no room for a letter, so pictures made today do not change. engine/testdata/frame-text.png shows it.
  • FrameBuffer.SetJoined(x, y, cell) stores a cell as Set does, except that a light box line or join (─ │ ┌ ┐ └ ┘ ├ ┤ ┬ ┴ ┼) drawn over another becomes the join with every arm that either has, in the colors of the one drawn: │ over ─ is ┼. Any other character, under or over, is replaced as by Set. A program drawing a table of lines of its own uses it, as DrawBox and widget.Panel now do.
  • Canvas.FillDisc(cx, cy, r, color) fills a disc whose center and radius can be fractions of a pixel, with a smooth edge, and Canvas.BlendDisc(cx, cy, r, color, t) mixes a color into one by the part t, for a shadow or a glow. FillCircle takes whole pixels, so a disc was an odd number of pixels across and moved a pixel at a time: a slow roll was a row of jumps, a radius of 1 a plus sign and 2 a small diamond, with nothing between. Pixel (x, y) is the square from (x, y) to (x+1, y+1), the coordinates of Camera.ProjectPixels, so a projected point is a center as it is. A pixel's share of the disc is how many of 64 points in it, on an 8 by 8 grid, are within r. In FillDisc a pixel the disc covers wholly is color, as FillCircle draws it, one on the edge is Mix(pixel, color, share), and an empty one, which has nothing to mix with, is color from half covered and stays empty below that. BlendDisc is Mix(pixel, color, share*t) and leaves empty pixels empty, as BlendRect does. A radius that is not positive or NaN, or a center that is not a finite number, draws nothing, and an infinite radius covers the canvas. The shares are counted the same way on every platform, so the same pixels are drawn everywhere. Neither reads or writes depth, and neither allocates. A disc takes about 1 microsecond at a radius of 2.5, 6 at 10.5 and 60 at 50.5, where FillCircle takes 0.03, 0.2 and 3.5 (BenchmarkCanvasDiscs).

Changed

  • DrawBox and widget.Panel join their borders with the light box lines already in the frame. Each drew its corners as if it were alone, so panels that share an edge, a list of rows, a table, a view split in two, showed ┌ where ├ belonged, and a program fixed each join by hand. Now a border drawn over a light line meets it as ├ ┤ ┬ ┴ ┼. A box or a panel alone looks as it did, and so does one drawn over anything else; a picture changes only where a border is drawn over a light line. DrawBox now writes each cell once and visits only the part inside the frame, and a size near the largest int no longer wraps around.
  • Canvas.BlendRect is 5 to 6% slower on arm64 than in 0.12.0, about 2 microseconds for a whole 200 by 120 canvas, because Mix now rounds the same way on every platform (see Fixed): each channel is a multiply and an add where arm64 fused them into one instruction. amd64 never fused them. make benchcmp BASE=v0.12.0 over the 216 benchmarks both versions have finds those four rows and DrawRect (+6 to 7%, which does not mix but moves with the same change) slower, Image 4 to 5% faster, and none that allocate more. Two more rows, DrawMany/pixels_200x60 and the scrolling log of BenchmarkFrames, were slower in one run, the same in the next, and slower again only once the Mix change was taken out, so they move with how the binary is laid out and not with any code they run.

Fixed

  • Run shows the frame on a terminal that reports 0 columns by 0 rows. It told the game 80 by 24 there, as the docs say, and drew the frame, but cut what it sent to the terminal's own 0 by 0, so the screen was erased and stayed empty: a recording or a smoke test made under script started from something that is not a terminal, in a CI job or in some container consoles, held nothing (#131). Now an axis the terminal reports as 0 shows the whole frame on it, 80 columns or 24 rows, or what Width or Height chose, until the terminal reports a size.
  • Mix and Canvas.BlendRect give the same colors on every platform. On arm64 the compiler fused the multiply and the add of each channel into one instruction, which rounds once where amd64 rounds twice, so a mix that lands next to a half went down on arm64 and up on amd64: Mix from red 1 to red 8 at a t just under 1/14 was red 1 on a Mac with Apple silicon and red 2 on an Intel one. The product is now rounded on its own on every platform, which is what amd64 did, so only arm64 changes, and only for those mixes, by one step of one channel.

0.12.0 - 2026-11-17

On top of 0.11.0 this release reads the rest of what a terminal sends: the function and navigation keys, the modifiers held with a key and the characters typed, a paste as one string, and the mouse. Nothing was removed or renamed and no signature changed; what behaves differently is under Changed, and reading keys costs a little more. The module needs the same versions of golang.org/x/sys and golang.org/x/term as 0.11.0.

Added

  • KeyHome, KeyEnd, KeyPageUp, KeyPageDown, KeyInsert, KeyDelete and KeyF1 to KeyF12 are keys, asked of Input like any other with IsDown, Pressed and Started, and pressed with Keyboard.Press in a test. A terminal sends them as longer sequences, which glonk read as nothing: the CSI n ~ ones were dropped as unknown, and the SS3 forms of F1 to F4, Home and End were skipped on purpose as not game keys, so a program that listened for them never heard. It now reads CSI n ~ with xterm's numbers (2 Insert, 3 Delete, 5 Page Up, 6 Page Down, 11 to 15 and 17 to 21 and 23 and 24 for F1 to F12) and with rxvt's for Home and End (1 and 7, and 4 and 8), CSI H and CSI F, CSI 1 ; modifiers then P, Q, R or S or those with no parameters, and the SS3 forms, and in the kitty keyboard protocol the repeat and the release of each. A modifier held with one of them is not looked at, as it is not on an arrow, so Ctrl+Page Down is Page Down; reading the modifiers is the next part of #18. CSI row ; column R is not F3: it is a cursor position report, which glonk does not ask for, and a first parameter other than 1 is not read as one of these keys. examples/input shows what a terminal sends, as glonk reads it, to try a terminal with: each key as it goes down, whether the terminal reports releases, and which keys are held. A program that used to see nothing for these keys now sees them.
  • Input.KeyEvents() is what happened to the keys in a frame, in order: each press, repeat and release, as a KeyEvent with the Key, its Mods (ModShift, ModAlt, ModCtrl, ModSuper, with Has) and an Action (ActionPress, ActionRepeat, ActionRelease). A shortcut such as Ctrl+S, a text field and Shift+Tab read it, and nothing is lost when three keys arrive in one frame, as it is when Pressed is a map. Input.Text() is the characters typed in the frame, in order, shift and layout applied, separate from the key presses: Enter, Tab, Backspace, the arrows, releases and chords type nothing there. Before, a terminal's Alt and Ctrl keys were dropped (Alt+x was read as nothing, and Ctrl and a letter as nothing), and a modifier held with an arrow or a function key was not looked at; they are now in KeyEvents. A chord, a character key with Ctrl, Alt or Super held, is in KeyEvents and not in IsDown, Pressed or Started, as it never was, so a game that walks on W is not walked by Ctrl+W, which a terminal may use to close a tab; and the arrows, Home, End, Page Up and Down, Insert, Delete and F1 to F12 are still the key whatever is held with them, as the arrows were. What a terminal says of the modifiers depends on it: with the kitty keyboard protocol they are exact; without it Shift is the case of a letter and not a modifier, Alt arrives as Escape and the key (so an Escape then a key within one read is Alt+key), Ctrl and a letter arrive as a control byte (so Ctrl+I is Tab, Ctrl+M is Enter and Ctrl+H is Backspace), and only the arrows, navigation and function keys carry their modifiers. Hyper and Meta chords are not read. A frame keeps at most 256 events and 4,096 bytes of text, which no person reaches.
  • Keyboard.PressWith(mods, keys...) presses a chord, and Keyboard.Type(text) types characters, so a game that reads KeyEvents and Text stays testable. A Press of a key that is already down is now an ActionRepeat in KeyEvents on a NewKeyboard, as the kitty protocol reports a repeat; Pressed and Started are as they were.
  • Input.Paste() is what was pasted since the previous frame, as one string, and Keyboard.Paste(text) pastes in a test. A terminal with bracketed paste marks a paste with ESC [ 200 ~ and ESC [ 201 ~, and glonk now asks for it, so a paste is one event and not typing: it is in no key event, in no map of keys and not in Text. The text is valid UTF-8 (U+FFFD for what is not), its line breaks are newlines (a terminal sends carriage returns), and at most 1 MiB is kept, in a paste and in a frame, with the rest dropped. The paste is collected across reads, with its end marker found wherever a read splits it, and a paste that stalls for half a second before its end is delivered as it stands, so that a broken one cannot swallow every key after it. A terminal without bracketed paste sends a paste as the key presses it is, as before, and Paste is empty.
  • Config.Mouse asks the terminal to report the pointer, with Input.MousePos() (the cell, from 0 at the top left of the terminal, and whether a report has said), Input.MouseDown(b), Input.MouseStarted(b) and Input.MouseReleased(b) for MouseLeft, MouseMiddle and MouseRight (a click shorter than a frame is started and released in it), and Input.Wheel() (what the wheel added up to in the frame: down and right are positive, at most 4,096 steps a frame on each axis). It is off by default, because a terminal that reports the pointer to a program stops selecting text with it. Run turns on every movement and the decimal report form (modes 1003 and 1006) with focus reporting, so that a button held when the window loses focus is let go, and turns them off when it gives the terminal back and across Ctrl-Z. Keyboard has MouseMove(x, y), MouseDown(b), MouseUp(b) and Wheel(dx, dy) for tests. Not reported: other buttons, modifiers, a position in pixels.

Changed

  • glonk asks a terminal with the kitty keyboard protocol for the text of each key (flag 16, with the three it asked for), which Input.Text reads, so that Shift, the layout and compose keys are right. A terminal that does not know the flag ignores it, and Input.Text then works the text out from the key: its character, with Shift applied to the ASCII letters only. A chord that a terminal says typed something is believed: Option and a is a with a ring on a Mac.
  • examples/input shows each key event with its modifiers and the text typed, as well as which keys are held.
  • A paste is no longer typed. Run asks the terminal for bracketed paste as it takes it over, and turns it off when it gives it back and across Ctrl-Z. Before, pasting into a program was typing: a newline pressed Enter, a pasted Ctrl-C ended the run, every escape sequence in it was read as keys, and a long paste was a long flood of key presses. Now, on a terminal with the mode, none of that happens, and the text is in Input.Paste for a program that wants it; a program that read pasted text off the key presses (Pressed of each letter) gets nothing from a paste until it reads Paste. A terminal without the mode is as it was.
  • A mouse report in the older form a terminal sends where it has no decimal mode, ESC [ M and three bytes, is dropped whole. Before, its three bytes were read as typed characters; no key sends ESC [ M, so nothing else is lost. A decimal report (CSI < ... M or m) was dropped before and is read now whether or not Config.Mouse was set, so a mouse mode that another program left on in the terminal moves MousePos.
  • Reading keys costs about 8 to 9 ns more a key than in 0.11.0, because a key event now carries its modifiers, a chord flag and its text (24 bytes where it was 8): 80 typed letters take 1.30 us where they took 0.64, and 20 arrow keys, or 20 keys of the kitty protocol, take 7 to 10% more, with the same allocations. Nobody types fast enough to notice. make benchcmp BASE=v0.11.0 over the 205 benchmarks both versions have finds 1 slower, BenchmarkImage scale_4 at +4.3%, in image code that did not change since 0.11.0 (when that row was checked against main in #97, it moved with the test code in the binary and not with the library), and none that allocate more.

0.11.0 - 2026-11-17

On top of 0.10.0 this release adds text drawn as pixels on the canvas, a way to put a rectangle of the canvas down over cells that are already in the frame, and a coin in the world example's panel, and it takes back the time that drawing a plain mesh lost in 0.10.0. Nothing was removed or renamed and no signature changed; the module needs newer versions of golang.org/x/sys and golang.org/x/term.

Added

  • Canvas.DrawText(x, y, text, color) and Canvas.DrawTextScaled(x, y, text, color, scale) draw text on a pixel Canvas in a pixel font that comes with glonk, and engine.PixelTextWidth(text, scale) says how wide it is. FrameBuffer.DrawText draws cells: on a screen that is otherwise pixels, a game's view or a HUD drawn on the canvas, cell text does not fit, since a letter is as tall as two pixels, cannot be larger, and replaces the pixels of the cells it is in. A game built on glonk drew its own alphabet of 5 by 7 letters as a picture and wrote a loop to stamp it, for the title and the menus. The font is the printable ASCII characters, space to ~, drawn for glonk and kept as data in the repo: letters 5 pixels wide and 7 tall, 6 apart, with every baseline on row 6. DrawTextScaled makes every pixel of a letter a block of scale by scale, for 2 or 3 times the size (a scale below 1 is 1, and one above 4096 is 4096). Only the pixels of the letters are written, so what is behind them shows between the strokes, and the zero Color erases them, as for FillRect; like all canvas drawing it never reads or writes depth. PixelTextWidth is the width from the left of the first letter to the right of the last, with no gap after it, so a title centers; the height is 7*scale. A character with no letter, an accent, a Chinese character, an emoji, a tab or a newline, is drawn as ?, as FrameBuffer.DrawText draws what it cannot show, a NUL is a space, and a character with no width of its own, a combining mark, is skipped and not counted. Lines are not wrapped. Only the part inside the canvas is visited, so a text far off it costs no more than the part that shows, and no position or size can overflow. A letter takes about 100 nanoseconds at the smallest size and 200 at three times, and nothing is allocated (BenchmarkCanvasDrawText).
  • Canvas.ComposeRect(x, y, w, h) is Compose for one rectangle of the canvas, in pixels, to put a picture, a shape or the pixel text of DrawText down over cells that are already in the frame. The canvas is under every cell, so a picture could not be inside a filled Panel: the fill replaced it, and calling Compose again put the whole view back over the panel. A program had to draw such pixels before Compose, leave the panel's Fill empty and fill the pixels behind them itself, so a picture and its frame were in two places. Now the panel is drawn, then the picture on the canvas, then ComposeRect with the picture's rectangle. Every cell the rectangle touches is put down, a cell being two pixels tall, so a picture on an odd pixel row works. A see-through pixel shows what the cell showed in that half: a half block keeps its other half, a space or a letter its background, and Reverse is read (a half the cell showed in the terminal's default foreground comes out in the default background). A cell that holds a letter loses it when one of its pixels is put down, since a half block cannot hold one, and a cell whose two pixels are both empty is not touched and keeps it. On a Seamless canvas a cell with a pixel is one solid color, as for Compose, and a cell takes the depth of its nearer pixel as Compose gives it. The canvas is not changed, and neither is Compose. Only the cells on the frame are visited, no position or size can overflow, and nothing is allocated (BenchmarkComposeRect). Where the canvas also holds a world that Compose has put down, Clear it before drawing the picture. examples/world shows a coin in its filled panel, which is 5 rows tall for it.

Changed

  • The module needs golang.org/x/term v0.48.0 and golang.org/x/sys v0.50.0, up from v0.47.0 and v0.49.0: the terminal layer, which engine uses for raw mode and the size of the window, and for the system calls under it. go get moves a program that pins older ones up to them. Nothing in the API comes from them.
  • make benchcmp BASE=v0.10.0 over the 199 benchmarks that the two releases have finds 1 slower, 26 faster and 0 that allocate more. The slower one is FillCircle of radius 50, by 4.6%, in code that this release does not change; the 0.9.0 notes recorded that row and tilemap Route.Steps, which is 7.1% faster here, moving with an unrelated file added to engine, and an unrelated function added there moves FillCircle from 3329 to 3510 ns, so both follow the layout of the binary and not the code. Of the faster ones, 25 are mesh drawing, by 3.6 to 10.9%, and meshes with a picture or that can be seen through draw 5 to 11% faster.

Fixed

  • Drawing a mesh with no picture and no opacity is as fast as it was in 0.9.0 again, which 0.10.0 said it was not: 3 to 6% slower, in 12 benchmarks (#86). There were two causes. For each face, 0.9.0 asked a plain mesh one question, whether it had vertex colors, and 0.10.0 asked three, for the picture, for the cut and for the colors; they are inside one test now, set once for the mesh, so a plain mesh skips all of them. And every helper that a mesh draw calls (offscreen, currentBounds, reach, worldNormals, lightsReaching and the two that walk the faces and the edges) took the whole Mesh by value, which grew from 240 to 280 bytes in 0.10.0, so a draw copied it several times; they take a pointer now, and the exported methods keep their value receivers and copy the mesh once, so nothing in the API changes. What did not matter: the bytes of the new fields on their own cost under 1% at the median, and neither the draw closures nor the opacity setup moved the numbers. make benchcmp BASE=v0.9.0 over the 48 benchmarks that found it finds 0 slower, and nothing allocates more. Against 0.10.0, meshes with a picture, and meshes that can be seen through, draw 5 to 11% faster in the benchmarks.

0.10.0 - 2026-11-16

On top of 0.9.0 this release adds pictures on the faces of a mesh, holes where a picture is see-through, meshes that can be seen through as glass is, and lines that lie on a surface. Material and Mesh have new fields, so a literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles; a model whose picture has transparent pixels is now drawn with holes in it; and drawing a mesh is 3 to 6% slower, which #86 is for. Nothing was removed or renamed.

Added

  • Material.Texture and Mesh.FaceTexCoords draw a picture on the faces of a mesh. A face was one color, Material.Color or its entry in FaceColors, so a picture on a flat wall needed the wall cut into one small face for each pixel of the picture: a first-person game built on glonk made 512 triangles of a wall that needs 2, and took 6 ms a frame for a small level. Texture is a *bitmap.Bitmap, and FaceTexCoords is a triple of coordinates for each face, one for each of its three corners, in the order of Faces, so a wall that shares its corners with the ones beside it needs no vertices of its own. (0, 0) is the top left of the picture and (1, 1) the bottom right, as Bitmap.At counts, and the picture repeats. DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels give every cell or pixel of such a face the nearest pixel of the picture at its own place on the face, worked out with the perspective-correct weights that smooth shading uses, so a picture is seen at a slant, and a face cut by the near or far plane keeps its coordinates. The picture's color is lit and faded in fog as a face color is, a vertex color tints it (each channel multiplied; a corner without one lets all of it through), and a see-through pixel is a hole: nothing is drawn there and no depth is stored, so what is behind shows through it, in whatever order things are drawn, as in a leaf or a fence (the rule bitmap has for alpha: 128 or more is drawn). A face with a coordinate that is not finite draws nothing. A zero triple means none, so a face can opt out. Validate reports coordinates that are not one triple for each face or are not finite, Clone copies them (and shares the picture), and wireframes ignore them. A wall of two triangles with a picture of 16 by 16 takes 7 microseconds in cells on 80 by 24, and 13 in pixels, where the same wall made of 512 faces takes 60 and 62 (BenchmarkDrawTextured).
  • FrameBuffer.FillTriangleOver and Canvas.FillTriangleOver fill a triangle like FillTriangleFunc, but their callback is given the cell or pixel that is already there and returns what to draw and a Fill: FillSkip leaves the cell and its depth as they were (a hole), FillSolid draws it and stores its depth as FillTriangleFunc does, and FillOver draws it and leaves the depth (see-through glass). They are what a surface that can be seen through is drawn with. The depth test comes first, as for FillTriangleFunc, so the callback is not asked about a cell that would be hidden. A cell whose centre is exactly on an edge is covered by the triangle that has the edge on its left or top and not by the one that has it on its right or bottom, so two triangles that share an edge cover it once between them, where FillTriangleFunc covers it for both: a surface made of triangles that is mixed over what is behind it, and stores no depth, would be mixed twice along every edge they share.
  • Material.Opacity makes a mesh see-through, as glass, water, smoke or a ghost is: the part of its color that shows, from 0 to 1, mixed over what is already drawn by engine.Mix, after the color is lit and faded in fog. A face was always solid, so such a thing had to be solid, or left out, or drawn by the program's own loop over the pixels. In pixels the pixel is mixed; in cells the character and attributes of the cell behind stay, and its foreground and background are each mixed, so glass over a blank cell is a block of its color. Over an empty pixel or a blank cell the color is mixed with black, as Mix reads the default color, so a ghost shows against the background. Such a face is tested against depth and does not store it: draw the solid meshes first, and then the see-through ones from far to near. glonk does not sort, between meshes or inside one. A face seen from behind is skipped unless DoubleSided, as a solid's is. 0, the default, and anything that is not between 0 and 1 mean solid, as Light.Intensity 0 means 1, so no mesh changes. A pane of two triangles over a wall takes 17 microseconds in cells on 80 by 24 and 26 in pixels, where the same pane solid takes 14 and 22 (BenchmarkDrawSeeThrough).
  • Mesh.DrawOnSurface and Mesh.DrawOnSurfacePixels, and FrameBuffer.DrawLineOnSurface and Canvas.DrawLineOnSurface, draw lines that lie on a surface that is already drawn: a grid on a floor, a box round the thing that is selected, a mark on a wall, the edges of a mesh over its own faces. DrawDepth hides such a line wherever it is a hair behind the surface, which across a floor seen at a low angle is half of it or all of it, and no lift fits every distance: a 1 by 1 outline on a floor, at 160 by 90 pixels and 1.5, 3, 5, 8 and 12 away, was kept in full only by a lift of a tenth of its size, where DrawOnSurfacePixels keeps all of it at every distance with none. A cell of a line is drawn if it is no farther than what is stored there plus the slope of that surface across a cell, read from the depths stored around it (the larger of the steps to the cells on each side along each axis, 1.5 times their sum), held to a quarter of the depth there, so a line well behind a surface, a solid in front of it, and the faces of a cube in front of its own far edges still hide it. The surface has to be drawn first. A line that lies on the surface leaves its depth as it was, and one in front of it keeps its own, as DrawDepth's does. A line a hair behind a surface that is seen edge-on shows, since no depth test can tell the two apart. The 12 edges of a cube take 2.5 microseconds in cells on 80 by 24, against 2.0 for DrawDepth (BenchmarkDrawOnSurface). DrawDepth, DrawLineDepth and Draw are as they were.

Changed

  • LoadOBJFS keeps a model's picture and its texture coordinates, in Material.Texture and Mesh.FaceTexCoords, so that a textured model is drawn in the picture at every cell and a large triangle is no longer one flat color. FaceColors is the same as it was, the color of the picture at the middle of each triangle, for a program that puts the picture aside (the viewer's C does). A mesh has one picture, the first that a triangle has coordinates in, and the triangles of a material with another picture keep their one color. 0.9.0 - 2026-11-15

On top of 0.8.0 this release adds a frame drawn as a picture, frame numbers for a running game, a bounding sphere for every mesh that lets drawing skip what is off screen and leave out the lights that cannot reach it, and a way to compare benchmarks with a base. A Mesh has a new unexported field, so a Mesh literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles; and a mesh from a shape or a loader now carries its bounds, so a program that changes its Vertices in place must call UpdateBounds, or the mesh can be skipped while it is on screen. Nothing was removed or renamed.

Added

  • FrameBuffer.Image(ImageOptions) draws a frame as an *image.RGBA, for a test to compare with a stored picture or for a person to look at without a terminal. String() gave the characters of a frame and no colors, and a frame drawn on a canvas is row after row of ▀, so a test could check where a letter was but not what the frame looked like; a game on glonk carried 130 lines of its own to turn cells into the pixels of a PNG, and the screenshots of #20 need the same. The picture is Width*Scale by 2*Height*Scale pixels, a cell being one pixel of a Canvas across and two down, so a frame made with a canvas comes out pixel for pixel at Scale 1 (a test checks it on random canvases). A half-block cell is its two pixels, a space or an unset character its background and █ its foreground; any other character is a mark, a rectangle in its foreground over the middle half of the cell, which shows where text is and in what color and not what it says. Reverse swaps a cell's colors; Bold, Dim and Underline are not drawn. ImageOptions has Scale (below 1 counts as 1), Colors (Colors256 shows RGB as the nearest palette color, as a terminal without true color does; the other modes are exact, since a picture has no terminal to ask) and Foreground and Background, what a cell's default colors look like, light gray and black unless set. A picture of more than 4,194,304 pixels is not made: the image is empty, 0 by 0, so a scale read from somewhere cannot ask for gigabytes. engine/testdata/frame.png is a real picture of a frame, compared pixel by pixel by a test (go test ./engine -update writes it again), and ExampleFrameBuffer_Image writes the picture to the path in GLONK_SCREENSHOT when it is set.
  • Stats and StatsReceiver tell a running game how its frames are doing. A game with a Stats(engine.Stats) method is called after every frame has been written, from the goroutine that calls Update and Draw, with FPS (frames a second, an average of about the last ten frames of the dt that Update is given, so a stall of more than a quarter of a second reads as 4), Update and Draw (the time of each; Draw includes clearing the frame), Write (working out which cells changed and writing them, so a terminal that cannot keep up shows here) and Bytes (everything sent for the frame, the sequences that move and set colors included; 0 when nothing changed, and the whole frame in a headless Run). Every program that showed a frame rate worked it out from dt, cube3d and the viewer with the same line, which says only the rate and not whether the time goes to Update, Draw or the terminal, and the bytes, which decide whether a program works over ssh (about 7 KB a frame for a moving 3D scene in half-block pixels, 66 KB for a frame where every pixel changes in 256 colors, 110 KB in true color, at 100 by 30), could not be known from inside a program at all. A game without the method is not timed: Run reads no clock for it, and a run of many frames allocates no more than one of few, with or without the method. It is not called for the frame whose Update ends the run, or after Run has returned. cube3d and the viewer show the numbers in place of their own rate line, with the times and the bytes on a line above the help.
  • A light is left out for a mesh that is wholly beyond its reach, so that a scene can have many lamps: a mesh with normals asked every light about every cell it drew, and a Point with a Range that gave 0 beyond it was asked all the same, so ten lamps in a level cost ten calls a cell everywhere though two or three reached any one room (a game kept the three nearest lamps, sorted every frame, to get round it). Point.Reach() says where the lamp is and how far it reaches (Position and Range, and infinity for a Range of 0, which does not fade), and a light of a program's own opts in with the same method, Reach() (center geometry.Vector3, radius float64), a ball outside which it gives nothing. A light is left out for a mesh whose sphere, the one UpdateBounds keeps, is farther from the ball than the two radii, by more than a margin for rounding of a billionth of the size of the numbers, and never when a number is not a number, a radius is negative or the mesh has no current bounds. Directional, Ambient and a light without Reach are always asked. Leaving out a light that gives 0 changes no sum, so the picture is bit for bit the same, which a test checks over random scenes of many lamps in every way a mesh is lit. Sixteen lamps along a corridor of ten smooth floor chunks, each lamp reaching a few of them: 40 microseconds in cells and 71 in pixels, down from 86 and 168; with every lamp reaching every chunk, where nothing can be left out, it costs about 1 percent more (1.2 and 0.7), the price of asking each lamp where it reaches. The lights are copied into the scratch for each mesh drawn (a pooled triangle that held the caller's list would put it on the heap), and drawing allocates nothing.
  • Mesh.UpdateBounds() works out and keeps the sphere that holds a mesh's vertices, which drawing uses to skip a mesh that is off screen. The sphere was always round Transform.Position and as wide as the vertex farthest from the model's origin: right for a model built round its origin and then placed, and wrong for a mesh whose vertices are written where they are in the world, as a level built in place is, whose sphere was round the world's origin and held everything, so that it was never skipped and nothing said so. A game found it with a level at 6 ms a frame, drawn whole with the half behind the player, and had to cut it into chunks and rewrite the vertices of each from its middle. The sphere is now round the vertices themselves, the middle of their box or the model's origin where that sphere is no bigger, and the Transform places it as it places them. It is kept in the Mesh, since working it out takes a pass over the vertices, which cost 12.3 microseconds for a sphere of 8,000 each time a mesh whose middle was off screen was tested. Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Plane, Grid, LoadOBJ, LoadOBJLimit, LoadOBJFS, Clone, SmoothNormals and Fit give a mesh its sphere, and a mesh built by hand has none until UpdateBounds, and is skipped as it was. It is used only while Vertices is the same list, with the same length and first element, so appending to it or replacing it can never make a stale sphere skip a mesh; a vertex changed in place needs UpdateBounds again, since a mesh that has moved out of its sphere can be skipped while it is on screen, and Validate reports a vertex outside it. A mesh of 8,000 vertices written in the world and behind the camera costs about 2.3 microseconds with the sphere, where it cost 71 for a wireframe and 297 for a shaded one without it; one placed by a Transform and behind the camera costs 2.2, down from 12.3. A model drawn on screen costs what it did. A Mesh literal that lists its values without field names, which no program should write, no longer compiles.
  • make bench runs every benchmark once with allocations, and make benchcmp compares the benchmarks of the working tree with those of a base commit, tag or branch (BASE=, origin/main unless said), for a change that might have made drawing, loading or searching slower, which was a check made by hand. It builds the test binaries of both without file paths, so that identical code is an identical binary, and runs them in rounds: the base, the base again and the head one after the other in an order that turns, so that a computer that warms up or gets busy affects all alike. The base run twice is a control for noise, which is about 0.3 percent for most benchmarks. A benchmark is reported as slower or faster only if both its best run and its typical run changed by more than a threshold (THRESHOLD=, 3 percent) and its best run by more than twice its noise; one whose noise alone is beyond the threshold is marked noisy; and any rise in allocations is a regression, since several benchmarks promise none, but for one of a thousandth or less in a benchmark that allocates hundreds of thousands of times. On identical code with that flag it flagged nothing in 151 benchmarks, and found a change of 5 percent in 147 of them. A package whose test binary is the same file as the base's has the same code, so it is not run and is listed as not run, which is exact, and a change to one package takes a minute or two, where all of them take about two minutes a round, ten at the default 5 rounds (FLAGS=-identical runs the rest too, to measure the noise). It exits 1 for a regression and 2 if it could not run. The tool is internal/benchcmp and needs only Go and git, and a bench workflow runs it in GitHub Actions when started by hand.

0.8.0 - 2026-11-15

On top of 0.7.0 this release adds pictures made in code and drawn in parts, mirrored, darker or in one color, a way to blend a color over the canvas, a kind for every tile of a tile map, line of sight and ray casts, and a way to walk to a target. A Tile has a new field, Kind, which FromLines fills, so a tile from it is no longer equal to the tile of its legend, and a Tile literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles.

Added

  • Route is the way to walk to a target over a Map, for the enemy that walks straight at the player and is stopped by the first wall between them. Route.To(m, targets...) searches the tiles that are not solid, breadth first, for how many steps each is from the nearest of one or more targets, Route.Next(from) gives the center of the neighbor tile that a mover at a point should step to, one step nearer, to steer to and Map.Move, and Route.Steps(p) is the number of steps. A step goes to any of the 8 neighbors of a tile, a diagonal one only if both tiles beside it are open, so that a mover does not cut through the corner of a wall, and a diagonal counts as one step, so the steps are not straight-line lengths; where several neighbors are one step nearer, a straight one is chosen before a diagonal one. A point is in the tile it floors to, as for Solid, and a target in a solid tile, outside the map, or NaN or infinite is left out. One Route serves every mover that wants the same target, so it is searched again when the target changes tile and not once a mover; it holds no pointer to the map, so it does not see tiles that change after the search, and it reuses its memory, so that a search allocates nothing once it has searched a map of that size. The work is a few steps for each tile at the most: a 400 by 200 map takes about 1.6 milliseconds with no walls and 2.8 with a fifth of it wall, a Next about 6 nanoseconds and a Steps about 4. Not included: tiles that cost more to cross, movers wider than a tile, obstacles that are not tiles, and a longest distance to search. The tests check every step against shortest paths found by Floyd and Warshall's algorithm over a graph built from the rule, and walk a box with Map.Move through mazes where one that goes straight at the target is stuck.
  • Map.Sees(a, b) says whether a straight line between two points crosses no solid tile, and Map.Cast(from, direction) shoots a ray and returns a Hit with the first solid tile it meets (X and Y), where it enters it (Point, on the tile's edge, the coordinate of the edge exact) and how far (Distance, in cells, a straight line), and whether there is one. Nearly every game on a tile map asks whether an enemy can see the player or what a shot hits, and a program that stepped along the line a tenth of a tile at a time and asked Solid could see past the tip of a corner and did far more work. Both walk the grid one tile at a time, exactly, so no wall is thin enough to miss and the work is at most the size of the map, and they share one walk, so they cannot disagree. A point is in the tile it floors to, as for Solid, Draw and sprites, so a point on the line between tiles is in the tile to its right or below it and a point at whole numbers, such as a sprite's position, is in its own tile alone, and a line through a corner is stopped only by the tile that holds the corner. Outside the map is solid, as for Move, so a ray from inside always hits something, at the edge of the map at the latest, as a tile outside it, and a ray that starts in a solid tile or outside the map hits at once with a distance of 0. The direction of a Cast can be any length but not 0, and a NaN or infinite number gives false. The walk is exact for numbers that a computer holds exactly, such as whole numbers, halves and sixty-fourths, which is what makes a corner a corner, and the tests check it against an exact oracle written with rational numbers. A 400 by 200 map crossed corner to corner takes about 1.4 microseconds, a short line about 33 nanoseconds, and none of it allocates.
  • Tile.Kind, a rune, says what a tile is, for a program to ask of Map.At: which picture a wall has, or whether a solid tile is a wall or a crate, which a Tile's look and Solid cannot say for a game whose tiles have no look and which kept the rows of its text beside the map only to ask. FromLines gives each tile of its legend the rune that made it as its Kind, unless the tile has a Kind of its own, which is kept. A rune the legend does not list, a space included, and the padding of a short line are still the empty tile, the zero Tile, with no kind: a program that wants to find a marker such as P lists it in the legend as 'P': {}. Two tiles that differ only in Kind are different tiles to the map, so runes whose tiles look alike are no longer one tile, which costs a place in the map's table of tiles and no more than the two bytes a cell it cost before. Two things change for a program that compared tiles: a tile from FromLines now has a Kind, so it is no longer equal to the tile of the legend that made it (compare its Cell and Solid, or its Kind, or give the legend tile a Kind), and a Tile literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles and must name them.
  • Bitmap.Shaded(brightness) and Bitmap.Tinted(color, part) return a view of a picture that is drawn darker, or mixed toward a color, as Sub and Mirrored return views of a part and of a reversal, so that a thing in a lit scene is as dark as the wall beside it, the letters of a font are drawn in any color from one white picture, and a hit flashes a thing white, where a program shaded or replaced the color of every pixel itself. A pixel of the view is drawn as engine.Shade(pixel, brightness) and then engine.Mix(that, color, part): a brightness of 1 and a part of 0 leave the colors as they are, a part of 1 draws the shape of the picture all in color, both are clamped to 0 to 1 with NaN as 0, the zero Color as the tint is read as black as Mix reads it, and a pixel that is see-through stays so. The shade comes first and the tint is laid over it, so a flash that is not shaded is not darkened by the room. A view has one brightness and one tint, so setting again replaces the old one instead of adding to it, and the order of the two calls does not matter. Sub and Mirrored keep the look. At and Set still deal in the picture's own colors, and a pixel that is drawn this way is an exact RGB color, as Shade and Mix make it, even at a brightness of 1 or a part of 0. Each is a Shade or a Mix for every pixel drawn, so a 64 by 64 picture shaded takes about 19 microseconds, tinted about 20 and both about 24, against 3.6 for one that is not; a picture that is not shaded or tinted is drawn as before, within about 3 percent, and drawing a view allocates nothing.
  • Bitmap.Mirrored() returns a view of a picture with its columns reversed, left to right, for a thing that faces the other way, as Sub returns a view of part of one: it shares the pixels, and everything sees the reversed picture, so its pixel (0, 0) is the picture's top right, At and Set count from the new left, Sub takes its rectangle from it, and Draw, DrawScaled and DrawScaledDepth draw it. Mirrored of a mirrored view is the picture the right way round again. Stretched it is the reversed picture stretched, which differs from a mirrored copy of the stretched picture only where the middle of a pixel is exactly between two pixels of the picture. Drawing a mirrored view allocates nothing, and Mirrored itself allocates the view.
  • Bitmap.Sub(x, y, w, h) returns a view of one rectangle of a picture, a Bitmap of its own that shares the pixels of the one it is taken from, so that a strip of animation frames in one file can be drawn a frame at a time: it can be drawn with Draw, DrawScaled and DrawScaledDepth, stretched and asked for its pixels like any picture, and Set on either changes the other. Nothing is copied, so a view keeps the whole picture in memory for as long as it is used, and the views are best made once at start-up. A rectangle that runs off the picture is cut to it, one with nothing left or with a width or height that is not positive is an empty picture of 0 by 0, and a Sub of a Sub is counted in the pixels of the first. No position or size can overflow. A Bitmap now has a row length of its own, so a view is not a run of the pixels behind it; drawing a whole picture is unchanged, and takes the same time as before to within about 3 percent, and drawing a view allocates nothing. Sub itself allocates the view, about 15 nanoseconds.
  • Canvas.BlendRect(x, y, w, h, color, t) blends color over the pixels of a rectangle by the part t from 0 to 1: each pixel that is there becomes Mix(pixel, color, t), so 0 leaves it and 1 makes it color. It is the canvas half of Mix: a dim behind a menu, a fade to black, a red flash and a see-through panel were each a loop of At, RGB, arithmetic and Set in every program, and the whole canvas is BlendRect(0, 0, c.Width(), c.Height(), ...). A pixel that is empty stays empty, since the terminal's own background is not known and there is nothing to mix with; a program that wants the tint there fills the pixels first. t outside 0 to 1 is clamped and NaN is 0, as for Mix, which does the arithmetic through the same function, so the two cannot disagree. A palette pixel becomes the exact RGB color it stands for, even at a t of 0, the zero Color as the color is read as black and does not erase, and depth is never read or written, as for FillRect. It clips as FillRect does, so a rectangle with a far-away edge costs no more than one on screen and no position or size overflows. It allocates nothing: a whole 200 by 120 canvas takes about 44 microseconds.
  • bitmap.New(w, h), Bitmap.Set(x, y, color) and bitmap.FromImage(img) make a Bitmap in code, where a Bitmap could only come from Decode, which reads a PNG or a JPEG, so that a program that draws a generated texture, a sprite in other colors or a small map of a level had to encode a PNG in memory and decode it again. New makes a picture of see-through pixels and Set gives one a color: the zero Color makes it see-through again, and a pixel outside the picture is left out, as for Canvas.Set. FromImage copies any image.Image with the rule Decode has for alpha, an alpha of 128 or more is drawn as an opaque color and less is not, and the top left of its bounds is pixel (0, 0); changing the image afterwards does not change the Bitmap. Decode's limits are for files that cannot be trusted, so FromImage has none, its pixels being in memory already; New gives an empty picture, 0 by 0, for a size that is not positive or has more than 4,194,304 pixels (2,048 by 2,048), as tilemap.New does, since a size can come from a file too. A picture with no pixels, from either, is safe to draw, as a nil one is.

0.7.0 - 2026-11-14

On top of 0.6.0 this release adds a far plane and fog to the camera, the ray through a place on the screen and the pixel of a point in the world, the lighting rule, pictures that stand in a 3D scene, and a way to tell a key's first press from its repeats. A Camera literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles, and widget.Menu chooses on a new press of Enter, not on a held one.

Added

  • Camera.Fog, a *Fog with a Color, a Start and an End, fades shaded meshes toward a color as they get deeper: DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels leave what is nearer than Start exactly as it is, make what is at End or beyond exactly the fog's color, and mix in a straight line between. In cells the color fades and the character, which shows the brightness, stays; in pixels the shaded color fades; flat and smooth, with and without vertex colors. A flat face is one color without fog, but with it every cell is its own depth, so each is asked for. The distance is the depth along Look, the same number as Far, Canvas.SetDepth, ProjectPixels and the t of a Ray; it is not the straight-line distance, so it does not change as the camera turns, and a thing at the side of the view is a little less foggy than one as far away in the middle. Fog.Apply(color, depth) is the same rule for a program that draws things of its own into the scene, so that they fade in step; a program that wants the world to end in the fog gives the camera a Far that End is at or before. A NaN Start, End or depth does nothing, and an End that is not beyond Start is a hard step at Start. Wireframes are not fogged. The Fog is a pointer, so copies of a Camera share it. With a fog, a big flat floor takes about 46 percent longer in cells and 65 in pixels, and a thousand cubes 6 and 15, since each cell and pixel is asked for its color; a camera with no Fog draws as it did, and the existing benchmarks are within about 1 percent of what they were, a thousand small meshes up to 3 percent for the 8 more bytes of the Camera.
  • engine.Mix(a, b, t) returns the color the part t of the way from a to b: a for 0, b for 1, and in between each of red, green and blue on a straight line, rounded to a whole number. t outside 0 to 1 is clamped and NaN is 0, as Shade does for an intensity, a palette color is read as xterm defines it and the default color as black, and the result is an exact RGB color. Shade moves a color toward black and nothing moved it toward another color. It is the first part of a way to blend colors; blending a color over a rectangle of a Canvas is not here yet.

A face or an edge that crosses it is cut where it crosses, as at the near plane, so a floor that runs on to the horizon ends at the plane; a face or an edge wholly beyond it is skipped, a face before it is lit, and a mesh wholly beyond it is not looked at, which is what saves the time to draw the far end of a scene. This is for solids and wireframes in cells and in pixels, and a point exactly Far away is drawn. Project and ProjectPixels report a point beyond it as not ok, so a program that draws its own things can leave them out too; Ray has no length, so it is not limited. A Far that is negative, NaN, or not beyond Near sees nothing, as for the other fields. A Camera literal that lists its values without field names, which no program should, no longer compiles and must name them. The drawing of a camera without a Far is the same picture. A thousand small meshes, which is mostly per-mesh work, take 3 to 5 percent longer for the bigger Camera; a big mesh takes no longer. With a Far of 20 over a scene 5 to 40 deep, a thousand cubes draw 13 to 17 percent faster, and a big sphere cut through its middle draws as a wireframe 39 percent faster and shaded no slower, since the far plane there takes away only faces turned from the camera.
  • Camera.Ray(x, y, w, h) and Camera.RayPixels(x, y, canvas) return the ray into the world through a place on the screen, the reverse of Project and ProjectPixels: the origin, which is the camera's Position, and a direction. The direction is one deep along Look, not a unit vector: its dot with Look is 1, so the point origin + t*direction is exactly t deep, the depth that Canvas.SetDepth and ProjectPixels use, and a program that fills a canvas ray by ray can store t as it is. Normalize gives a unit vector, and then t is the straight-line distance. The ray through the middle of the screen is Look, a place off the screen has a ray too, and the ray through the middle of pixel (i, j) is RayPixels(i+0.5, j+0.5, canvas). ok is false for a camera that sees nothing, a grid with no height, a place that is not a number, a nil canvas, and a position, yaw or pitch that is not a number. This is what picking, placing something where the player points, and drawing by rays need, without a copy of the projection out of the source. The tests check it against what DrawShadedPixels draws, pixel by pixel.

  • Bitmap.DrawScaledDepth(canvas, x, y, w, h, depth) is DrawScaled for a picture of a thing that stands in a 3D scene: each pixel it draws is stored with Canvas.SetDepth, so a nearer surface hides it, it hides a farther one, in whatever order they are drawn, and a tie keeps what is there, as for Canvas.DrawLineDepth. Draw and DrawScaled still never read or write depth, which is right for a logo or a HUD. The depth is one number for the whole picture, the distance along the camera's view direction, as Camera.ProjectPixels gives it with the pixel to draw at. A see-through pixel leaves the color and the depth alone, a depth that is not positive, or is NaN, draws nothing, and an infinite depth is infinitely far, so nothing is drawn. Everything else is as for DrawScaled: the same clipping, the same exact scaling (it is checked to draw what DrawScaled draws on an empty canvas at every position and size, the extremes of an int included), and no allocation. A 64 by 64 picture stretched to 128 by 96 takes about 29.6 microseconds, 4 percent more than DrawScaled, and one over the whole 200 by 120 canvas about 59 against 56.
  • mesh.Brightness(lights, point, normal) returns how bright a list of lights makes a surface at a point with a normal, from 0 to 1: the rule DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels light a face with, which was not exported before. Each light fills part of what the ones before it left dark, a light's own result outside 0 to 1 is clamped, NaN counts as 0, a nil light gives nothing, and no lights give 0. A program that draws things of its own into a lit scene can call it to light them as the meshes are lit, without a copy of the rule from the docs that goes wrong when the rule changes. The drawing calls the same function.
  • Camera.ProjectPixels(p, canvas) says where a point of the world is on a Canvas, in fractional pixel coordinates to floor with math.Floor, and how deep it is, the way DrawShadedPixels, DrawPixels and DrawDepthPixels place and store what they draw: Project on the grid the canvas belongs to with y stretched to pixel rows, and the distance along Look as the depth. A program that draws its own markers, billboards or particles into the canvas can then give Canvas.SetDepth or Canvas.DrawLineDepth the same pixel and depth, instead of finding both in the source. It is not ok where Project is not, and for a nil canvas; a point off the canvas is still projected. Project is unchanged.
  • Input.Exact() says whether the terminal reports key releases, so that IsDown and Started are exact, or whether held keys are the timing guess and only one key stays held at a time. A game that needs two keys at once can use it to warn the player, or to change to controls that work one at a time. It is true on a terminal with the kitty keyboard protocol and in a headless Run, unless Config.LegacyKeys is set, and it is settled before the first Update.

Keyboard.Press of a key that is already down is a repeat: pressed again, not started. The docs of Pressed say that it fires on repeats.

On top of 0.5.0 this release adds widgets for a heads-up display and menus, rectangles and circles on the canvas, and pictures from JPEG and PNG files, and reads the terminal size from the output so that the examples start on Windows. Nothing was removed, renamed or changed.

Added

  • Canvas.FillRect(x, y, w, h, color) fills a rectangle of pixels and Canvas.DrawRect draws its border, one pixel thick and inside it, so a rectangle under 3 pixels wide or tall is all border. A width or height that is not positive draws nothing. Like DrawLine they clip, never read or write depth, and let the zero Color erase, and only the part on the canvas is visited, so no position or size can overflow or make them slow. A whole 200 by 120 canvas takes about 9 microseconds, a small rectangle 85 nanoseconds and a border about 0.5 microseconds, none allocating.
  • Canvas.FillCircle(cx, cy, r, color) fills the disc of radius r around a pixel, the pixels with (x-cx)² + (y-cy)² <= r², and Canvas.DrawCircle draws its outline: the disc's pixels that have a neighbor above, below, left or right outside it, a ring one pixel thick. A negative radius draws nothing, a radius of 0 is one pixel and a radius over 2^29 is taken as 2^29. The row widths come from an exact integer square root, so the same pixels are drawn on every platform. Clipping, depth and the zero Color are as for rectangles. A disc of radius 50 takes about 3.6 microseconds, its ring 1, one that covers the whole canvas 9.7 and one far away 2 nanoseconds, none allocating.
  • A new package, bitmap, reads a JPEG or PNG and draws it on a Canvas. Decode(r) finds the kind by what the data holds and turns the picture once into the colors of a canvas; Bitmap.Draw(canvas, x, y) draws it at its own size and Bitmap.DrawScaled(canvas, x, y, w, h) stretches or squeezes it, taking for each pixel the one pixel of the picture under its middle, with no blending, exactly even for the largest sizes. Width, Height and At read it, and every method is safe on a nil *Bitmap. A pixel with an alpha of 128 or more is drawn as an opaque color and a lighter one is left alone, so what is on the canvas shows through; black is a color, and a JPEG has no alpha, so all its pixels are drawn. Drawing clips, never reads or writes depth, visits only the pixels on the canvas and allocates nothing: 64 by 64 pixels take about 3.7 microseconds, scaled to 128 by 96 about 29, and one far off the canvas 0.3. Decoding a 64 by 64 PNG takes about 81 microseconds and allocates once for each pixel.
  • bitmap.Decode refuses a file over 32 MiB, or one whose header says it has more than 4,194,304 pixels (2,048 by 2,048), before decoding it, with ErrTooLarge, and data that is not a JPEG or PNG with ErrFormat. bitmap.DecodeLimit(r, maxBytes, maxPixels) takes limits of your own, a limit that is not positive refuses every picture, and a source that never ends is read only up to the limit. The decoders are linked only into programs that import bitmap.
  • A new package, widget, draws the pieces of a heads-up display and a menu in screen space. Each widget is a value with exported fields and a Draw method that takes a position and a size in whole cells, as DrawBox does. What runs off the frame is cut off, a size that is not positive draws nothing, and numbers as large as an int cannot wrap around onto the frame. Drawing allocates nothing. A style is an engine.Cell whose colors and attributes are used and whose character is not.
  • widget.Label writes a line of text aligned Left, Center or Right in a width, counted in cells as TextWidth counts them and cut off where the width ends, with a combining mark kept with its character. Only the cells of the text are drawn.
  • widget.Panel draws a border with a title on the top edge, cut to fit and shown from 7 cells wide, and fills the inside with Fill. A blank Fill with no background and no attributes, which the zero value is, is a hole that leaves the inside alone. A panel smaller than 2 by 2 draws nothing, as DrawBox does.
  • widget.Bar draws a value from 0 to 1 as a run of cells filled from the left, rounded to the nearest cell. A smaller value is 0, a larger one is 1 and NaN is 0. Full and Empty are the cells, █ and ░ where they are the zero Cell, and are drawn as they are.
  • widget.Menu is a list of items with one selected. Update(in) moves the selection on Up and Down, wrapping around at the ends, and on Enter returns the index of the item and true; Escape and every other key are left to the game. Draw puts an item on each row, cut to the width, with Reverse toggled across the selected row, and scrolls a list longer than its rows to keep the selected item in the middle row. A Selected that is not an item is taken as 0, and Draw does not change the menu, which keeps no state of its own beyond Selected.
  • engine.TextWidth(text) returns the number of cells FrameBuffer.DrawText fills with a string, not counting any it would clip: one for each character except combining marks and joiners, which have none. A wide or control character is one cell, drawn as ?. It is for centering, right-aligning or cutting text where DrawText would put it.

Changed

  • examples/world has a coin panel at the top left, with the count and a bar that fills as coins are collected, and a pause menu: Esc now opens a menu to resume or quit, with the world standing still, instead of quitting at once.

0.5.0 - 2026-11-11

On top of 0.4.0 this release adds 2D collision, a tile map and a camera that follows the player. Nothing was removed, renamed or changed.

Added

  • A new package, tilemap: a grid of tiles for a game to be played on. Tile is an engine.Cell and whether it is Solid. FromLines(legend, lines...) makes a Map from text art, New(w, h, fill) from one tile, and Width, Height, Bounds, At, Set and Solid read and change it, safe outside the map and on a nil one. Outside the map is solid: the edge of the world is a wall. A map holds up to 4,194,304 cells and 65,535 kinds of tile, at two bytes a cell, and a bigger one is empty.
  • Map.Draw(fb, view) draws the part of a map that is in a geometry.Rect, the camera, using only its top left corner, floored in the map. A tile that is a hole (blank, no background, no attributes) is not drawn and shows what is under it, so one map can be laid over another. 200 by 60 cells take about 20 microseconds, with no allocation.

It looks at every tile along the way, so a long move cannot carry the box through a thin wall, and it stops with the box's edge exactly on the tile's edge, so there is no rounding gap and the box rests against a wall the same at any frame rate. It returns the moved box and whether it was stopped on each axis, takes 24 nanoseconds and allocates nothing, and its work is bounded by the size of the map whatever the delta. A box that starts inside a solid tile can move on, one outside the map cannot move, and an empty box or a NaN or infinite number is not moved.
  • Layer.DrawView(fb, view) draws a sprite layer as seen through a Rect: each sprite goes to the cell its position is floored to, minus the cell the view's corner is floored to, both floored in the world first, so that sprites and tiles never slip against each other as the view moves. Layer.Draw is untouched and measures the same.
  • Rect.Center, Rect.CenterOn(p) and Rect.Within(bounds) make a camera out of a rectangle: the screen's, centered on the player and kept inside the level (or centered on an axis where the level is smaller than the screen).
  • examples/world: a level of 120 by 36 cells written as text art, a camera that follows the player, walls and water that stop and slide it, and spinning coins to collect. make run EXAMPLE=./examples/world.
  • geometry.Rect, an axis-aligned rectangle of the points from Min up to but not including Max, half-open like the cells a sprite covers, so rectangles that only touch do not overlap. NewRect, Width, Height, Size, Empty, Contains, Overlaps, Intersect and Translate. A rectangle with no area, turned inside out or with a NaN in it is empty and overlaps nothing.
  • Rect.PushOutOf(o): the smallest move, along one axis, that takes a rectangle out of another, for walls and paddles. It picks the axis on which they overlap least and the way that is shorter (ties toward larger X, then Y, and X before Y), and is the zero vector when they do not overlap or when no finite move would do. It does not look at where the mover came from, so one that moves farther than its own size in a frame can come out the far side of a thin wall; after a push the boxes touch to within rounding, so round a pushed position (to a millionth, say) or it can be drawn a cell short.
  • Sprite.Bounds is the box a sprite covers, from its Position as it is (not floored) by the size of the animation's current frame, else the frame, else 1 by 1, holes included; a sprite with nothing to draw, or at a NaN or infinite position, has an empty box. Sprite.Overlaps asks whether two boxes share area.
  • Layer.All and Layer.Hits(r) range over the sprites of a layer, and over those whose boxes overlap a Rect, in draw order. They allocate nothing (1,000 sprites take about 4 microseconds to ask), the layer must not change while they run, and Layer.Draw is as fast as before.
  • examples/sprites has a rock in the sea. The ship is pushed out of it, so steering into it stops the ship against its face and steering in at an angle slides it along; the ghost floats through. The ship's step is capped at 0.05 s so that a long frame cannot carry it through the rock.

0.4.0 - 2026-11-10

On top of 0.3.0 this release colors models: from the pictures their materials name, and from colors on their vertices. Nothing was removed or renamed.

Added

  • Mesh.VertexColors: a color for each vertex, drawn as the blend of a face's three corner colors at every cell, with the perspective-correct weights smooth shading uses for normals, flat or smooth, in cells (DrawShaded) and in pixels (DrawShadedPixels). A face cut by the near plane blends as the whole face would. A vertex color multiplies the color of a face that has one (FaceColors: a material's Kd or texture), so white leaves it as it is; a face without one is drawn in the vertex colors as they are. A corner with the zero Color takes what the face would be without vertex colors, and a default color there reads as black. A face none of whose corners has a color is drawn as before. A list of any other length is not used and Validate reports it; Clone copies it, Fit and SmoothNormals keep it, and wireframes ignore it.
  • LoadOBJ, LoadOBJLimit and LoadOBJFS read the vertex colors of v x y z r g b lines: exactly six numbers, 0 to 1 and clamped as Kd is, black a color. Any other count, or numbers that are not understood, gives none and never fails the load. The list starts at the first colored vertex, so a file without colors has none, and a vertex copied for its normals keeps its color.
  • Color.RGB returns a color's red, green and blue (a palette color as xterm defines it, the default color as black), so that colors can be mixed outside the engine.
  • The viewer has a bundled octahedron with a color on each corner, between the icosphere and the torus. It opens in its own colors, and C puts them aside for the palette and back, as it does for the house.
  • Texture colors on models. LoadOBJFS reads the map_Kd picture of a material (JPEG or PNG, found by its content, beside the OBJ) and the texture coordinates of the faces (vt), and colors each triangle with the nearest pixel of the picture at the middle of its three corners' coordinates, in Mesh.FaceColors: the picture repeats, v runs from the bottom, transparency is ignored and colors are not gamma converted, as for Kd. A triangle without usable coordinates, or whose picture could not be had, keeps the material's Kd, and a material with both shows the picture. One color for each triangle means a large one is flat; a model of small triangles looks textured. Only the plain map_Kd name form is read (not -s, -o and the like), and no other map.
  • The picture is as untrusted as the model, and a problem with it never fails the load. Its size is read from the header and refused before decoding: 4096 by 4096 pixels (16,777,216) may be decoded per model in all, including a picture that then fails; 8 pictures are tried; a file is read up to 64 MiB or maxBytes. A picture is read when the first face that uses it comes, once. While loading, a decoded picture is held (up to 128 MiB, freed after) and 16 bytes for each vt line.

Changed

  • mesh.Mesh has a new field, VertexColors. Code that sets fields by name is unaffected, and a mesh without it draws as before. A Mesh{...} literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles, and go vet already warns about those: name the fields.
  • LoadOBJ and LoadOBJLimit now read the colors of six-number vertex lines, which they used to ignore, so a model that carried colors loads with them. Reading such a line takes about 77 ns longer (a file of 60,000 colored vertices loads 27% slower); a file without colors loads in the same time and with the same allocations.
  • Mesh is 24 bytes larger and is copied for every draw, so the shaded draw benchmarks take 1 to 4% longer (the largest on a scene of 200 small meshes; the wireframe benchmark, whose code did not change, moved 1 to 2% in the same runs). Drawing a mesh without vertex colors gives the same cells and allocates nothing, as before.
  • A model that used to load with no colors, or some, may now load with all: the pirate ship test model went from 1,552 colored faces of 29,860 to all of them, and a model of 800,000 faces from none to all. Loading takes longer by the time to decode the picture (23 to 61 ms, and 615 to 844 ms for the two), and LoadOBJFS of a file with many vt lines and no texture takes about 17% longer to read them. LoadOBJ and LoadOBJLimit are unchanged and read no vt.
  • Programs that import mesh now link the standard library's image/jpeg and image/png decoders, about 320 KB more in the viewer (3.08 to 3.40 MB); they also register those formats with image.Decode in the program, as importing them always does.

0.2.0 - 2026-11-08

The module path is now glonk.dev, which is a breaking change: see Changed, and "Moved to glonk.dev" in the README for the commands that move a project. On top of 0.1.0 this release adds smooth shading, true color, depth-tested wireframes and colors on models, and sends the terminal far less per frame.

Added

  • Smooth shading. Mesh.Normals, one normal per vertex in model space, makes DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels light every cell instead of every face, from the position and normal blended across the face with perspective-correct weights; a mesh without Normals is lit as before. A Light is then asked about the place on the surface and the unit normal there. A zero normal means none here and takes the face's own; normals turn with the Transform and ignore its scale; a list of the wrong length or with a number that is not finite is not used (the mesh is shaded flat) and Validate reports it. A back face of a DoubleSided mesh uses the flipped normal, and near-plane clipping blends the position and normal at the cut. Smooth shading costs about 12 to 23% more than facets on a scene of spheres; flat meshes pay nothing.
  • Mesh.SmoothNormals returns a copy with normals found by averaging the faces that share each vertex, weighted by the angle each makes there, so cutting a surface differently does not change how it is lit. Clone and Fit carry Normals.
  • Sphere, Cylinder, Cone and Plane: solid meshes with normals, hard edges where a surface has one (the caps of a cylinder, the base of a cone), and wireframe edges that follow the form. Segments are brought into 3 to 512 and rings into 2 to 512, a size that is negative or not finite counts as 0, and the result always passes Validate.
  • Point, a light from one place that fades with distance: Intensity (0 means 1) times how squarely the surface faces it times (1 - distance/Range) squared. Range 0 means no fading; a negative Range gives no light.
  • LoadOBJ reads vn and the normal index of faces (v//vn, v/vt/vn, negative indices). A place that a file lights with several normals is split into one vertex for each, so a cube comes back with 24 vertices; edges still join the original places. A normal index that is missing, not a number or names no normal read so far leaves the corner without one instead of failing the load, as texture indices always have been ignored. A vn statement that is malformed is an error naming the line.
  • FrameBuffer.FillTriangleFunc and Canvas.FillTriangleFunc fill a triangle by asking a function for each covered cell, with the perspective-correct weights of the three corners there. The depth test runs first, so the function is never asked about a hidden cell.
  • The viewer shows a sphere, a cylinder and a cone after the OBJ samples. F switches the current model between smooth and faceted (a model without normals is smoothed from its faces), and L switches the light between the sun and a lamp that follows the camera. The HUD names the shading and the light.
  • Mesh.DrawDepth, a wireframe with depth: each cell of each edge is stored with its distance, so a nearer surface hides it, it hides a farther one, and the order of drawing does not matter. Draw is unchanged and still paints over everything. Strictly nearer wins and a tie keeps what is there, so a line lying exactly on a surface flickers; there is no bias. Solids drawn into a Canvas hide it once Compose has run.
  • FrameBuffer.DrawLineDepth draws the cells DrawLine does, each stored with SetDepth, with a perspective-correct depth between the two ends (1/z runs evenly along the line); a depth that is not positive draws nothing.
  • cube3d has a seventh cube, a cyan wireframe drawn with DrawDepth: the solid cubes hide the part of its edges they cover, in character and half-block modes alike.
  • Mesh.FaceColors: a color for each face, or none, that replaces Material.Color for that face in DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels, flat or smooth, with the brightness still shown by the character or the shade. A zero entry takes Material.Color; a list of any other length is not used and Validate reports it; Clone, Fit and SmoothNormals keep it. A mesh without it draws as before, with no allocation and within 1% of the time on the benchmark scenes.
  • LoadOBJFS(fsys, name, maxBytes) loads an OBJ from an fs.FS with the colors of its materials: mtllib names MTL files beside it, and usemtl picks the Kd for the faces after it (a polygon gives all its triangles the color). Only Kd is read, with no gamma conversion. A name that is not a plain path inside the OBJ's folder, a file that is missing, broken or over 1 MiB (or maxBytes), and an unknown material never fail the load: the faces come out with no color. At most 8 files and 10,000 materials are read per OBJ. LoadOBJ and LoadOBJLimit still skip materials. Loading a file with no materials takes the same time and memory as before.
  • The viewer's house has a house.mtl and opens in its own colors. C goes from them through the six palette colors and back; a model without colors steps the palette as before. A file given on the command line is colored by the .mtl beside it. The HUD says model colors while they show.
  • True color. An RGB color is sent exactly (ESC[38;2;r;g;b m) when COLORTERM is truecolor or 24bit or TERM names true color, and as the nearest palette color otherwise, as before. Config.Colors (ColorsAuto, Colors256, ColorsTrue) overrides the detection, for tmux and ssh where COLORTERM often does not arrive. Palette colors are never sent as RGB, so the user's theme still applies to them. A headless Run uses 256 colors unless ColorsTrue is set, so what it writes never depends on the environment.

Changed

  • Breaking: the module path is now glonk.dev, where up to 0.1.0 it was github.com/kyrokohan/glonk. Imports become glonk.dev/engine, glonk.dev/mesh, glonk.dev/geometry and glonk.dev/sprite. The code is still hosted on GitHub; glonk.dev tells the go command where. Nothing else in the API changed because of this.
  • The viewer reads OBJ files up to 128 MiB, where it stopped at 64, so a model of 800,000 triangles (81 MB) loads.
  • A model file whose faces refer to normals now comes back with Normals, and so is drawn smooth where it used to be drawn flat. A file whose faces use no normals loads as it did, with no Normals, in the same time and with a little less memory (93 MB for the 3.7 MB benchmark file, 20% fewer allocations). Memory use is roughly 25 times the input size, up to about 35 for a file that gives most corners a normal of their own; LoadOBJLimit now says 35.
  • Drawing a mesh allocates nothing once warm, where it took 7 to 40 allocations a draw, and is up to about 20% faster on the benchmark scene. The camera's trigonometry is worked out once per draw instead of once per vertex, with the same arithmetic in the same order, so no picture moved.
  • Canvas.Compose also records depth: each cell it fills gets the depth of its nearer pixel in the FrameBuffer, in place of the depth it had. Anything drawn into the cells afterwards with a depth (FillTriangle, DrawLineDepth, SetDepth) is now hidden behind pixels that are nearer; before, it always landed on top. Drawing without a depth (Set, DrawLine, DrawText) is unaffected, and the cells Compose writes are the same. A pixel set with Set has no depth and counts as infinitely far. Compose takes about 10 microseconds longer on a full 200x60 canvas.
  • Drawing a mesh starts by asking whether any of it can be on screen, and skips it if not, before it is placed, transformed or lit. No picture changes. A scene of a thousand cubes with 4% in view draws 58 to 75% faster, and a large mesh behind the camera 80 to 95% faster; scenes with everything in view are within noise.
  • Shade returns an exact RGB color for every kind of color (a palette color is read as xterm defines it, the default color as black), so shades are finer on a true color terminal. On a terminal with 256 colors the shades look as before; about 2% of them land on the neighboring palette color, always one as near to the true shade. Code that compared Shade(Palette(n), x) with a palette color needs to change.
  • Run sends a terminal only the cells that changed since the last frame, as one synchronized update (terminal mode 2026, which terminals that do not know it ignore), instead of every cell of every frame. A screen that does not change writes nothing, and the example programs write 74% to 100% fewer bytes. The screen is drawn again in full after a resize, after Ctrl-Z, after a failed write and when the terminal reports the window regained focus. A headless Run still writes every frame whole. Text that something else prints to the terminal during a run is no longer overwritten by the next frame.
  • Escape sequences are built without fmt into a buffer that is reused from frame to frame, and a change of colors or attributes is written as one sequence. A frame takes 2 to 7 times less time to produce, allocates nothing once running, and a color-heavy frame is up to 9% smaller.

0.1.0 - 2026-11-08

The first release. The API is pre-1.0 and may still change. The README lists the known limits of what exists today.

Added

engine

  • Run drives a Game at a fixed frame rate (1 to 1000) and owns the terminal: alternate screen, raw mode, restored on return, on errors and on panics in game code. The frame fills the terminal and follows resizes, or has a fixed size and shows the part that fits.
  • FrameBuffer of Cells with a Foreground and Background Color and text Attrs (Bold, Dim, Underline, Reverse); DrawLine, DrawBox, DrawText, and FillTriangle with a depth buffer.
  • Color is a type: the terminal default (the zero value), Palette(n) for the 256-color palette, or RGB(r, g, b). RGB is shown as the nearest palette color until true color output is added.
  • Canvas: a pixel layer with two pixels per cell, composed into half-block characters, with a Seamless mode for terminals that draw seams. Shade maps a color to a brighter or darker one.
  • Keyboard Input with held keys and one-shot presses. Terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol report real key releases, so several keys can be held at once; everywhere else held keys fall back to timing. Escape sequences split across reads are reassembled, a lone Escape counts after 50 ms, Alt chords are ignored, and keys typed while glonk starts up are kept.
  • Config.Input and Config.Output (default os.Stdin and os.Stdout) for running on another terminal such as /dev/tty, or on none: with an input or output that is not a terminal, Run runs headless at the given Width and Height, writes only the frames, and leaves terminal settings and signals alone, so a game can be tested with key bytes in and a buffer out. With neither a terminal nor a size, Run returns an error that says so.
  • Resizer: a game with a Resize(width, height int) method is told the frame size before the first Update and whenever it changes.
  • A written contract for how Run ends and what it calls: it returns nil when the player quits (Ctrl-C, ErrQuit, a quit signal) and the context's error (context.Canceled, context.DeadlineExceeded) when the context ends it, at once if the context is already done; Resize, Update and Draw are called one at a time from one goroutine, none starts once the context is done, and none is called after Run returns.
  • Test helpers: Keyboard presses and releases keys for a game's Update (exact, like a terminal that reports releases, with no clock), and FrameBuffer has a String method, so a whole screen can be compared as text and fmt.Println(fb) prints it.
  • Terminal safety: SIGINT, SIGTERM and SIGHUP end Run cleanly (a second signal kills a stuck game); Ctrl-Z suspends and fg resumes; Run leaves stdin alone once it returns; line wrapping is off while a program runs.
  • Output safety: every cell is checked before it is sent, so control characters and escape sequences become ?, wide characters become ?, combining marks are skipped by DrawText, and NUL is a space.

mesh

  • Mesh with edges and triangular faces; Cube, Grid, LoadOBJ and LoadOBJLimit (which bounds untrusted input); Fit, Bounds, Validate and Clone.
  • Transform places a mesh: Position, Yaw, Pitch, Roll and a uniform Scale (0 means 1), in Mesh.Transform. Yaw and Pitch turn a model the way they turn a Camera: positive yaw swings the front toward +x, positive pitch tips it up. Transform.Apply says where a model point ends up. A transform with a number that is not finite, or a negative scale, draws nothing and Validate reports it.
  • Material says how a mesh looks shaded: a Color and a Ramp of brightness characters (default .:-=+*#%@), in Mesh.Material; the draw calls no longer take a color.
  • Light is an interface with Directional and Ambient lights, and your own; DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels take any number. Lights add up without passing full brightness, so one ambient light and one directional light give what the single combined light gave before.
  • Perspective Camera with yaw, pitch, field of view, a near plane and a CellAspect (how much taller than wide a cell is; fields left at zero use defaults), and Project, Forward, Right and Look for putting things on screen and moving around; wireframe Draw; DrawShaded and DrawShadedPixels with depth testing, back-face culling and near-plane clipping; DoubleSided for models wound the other way.
  • Drawing does not panic on bad meshes, transforms or cameras: edges and faces that name a missing vertex are skipped, and a camera with nonsense values sees nothing.

sprite, geometry

  • Sprite is an engine.Cell (character, colors, attributes) at a geometry.Vector2 position, drawn in a Layer that can Add, Remove, Clear and count (Len) its sprites; a sprite replaces the whole cell under it. Vector2 and Vector3.

Everything else

  • Three example programs (bounce2d, cube3d and a model viewer), runnable examples in the package documentation, fuzz tests for the parsers, rasterizers and clipping, and a test that keeps the documentation complete.
  • Continuous integration (format, vet and race tests on Linux and macOS for Go 1.26 and 1.27, cross-compilation, 32-bit tests), Dependabot, a security policy and contributing notes.
