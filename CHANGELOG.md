# Changelog

All notable changes to glonk are written here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). While the version is below 1.0, a new minor version (0.2.0, say) may change the API.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `Canvas.FillRect(x, y, w, h, color)` fills a rectangle of pixels and `Canvas.DrawRect` draws its border, one pixel thick and inside it, so a rectangle under 3 pixels wide or tall is all border. A width or height that is not positive draws nothing. Like `DrawLine` they clip, never read or write depth, and let the zero `Color` erase, and only the part on the canvas is visited, so no position or size can overflow or make them slow. A whole 200 by 120 canvas takes about 9 microseconds, a small rectangle 85 nanoseconds and a border about 0.5 microseconds, none allocating.
- `Canvas.FillCircle(cx, cy, r, color)` fills the disc of radius r around a pixel, the pixels with (x-cx)² + (y-cy)² <= r², and `Canvas.DrawCircle` draws its outline: the disc's pixels that have a neighbor above, below, left or right outside it, a ring one pixel thick. A negative radius draws nothing, a radius of 0 is one pixel and a radius over 2^29 is taken as 2^29. The row widths come from an exact integer square root, so the same pixels are drawn on every platform. Clipping, depth and the zero `Color` are as for rectangles. A disc of radius 50 takes about 3.6 microseconds, its ring 1, one that covers the whole canvas 9.7 and one far away 2 nanoseconds, none allocating.
- A new package, `bitmap`, reads a JPEG or PNG and draws it on a `Canvas`. `Decode(r)` finds the kind by what the data holds and turns the picture once into the colors of a canvas; `Bitmap.Draw(canvas, x, y)` draws it at its own size and `Bitmap.DrawScaled(canvas, x, y, w, h)` stretches or squeezes it, taking for each pixel the one pixel of the picture under its middle, with no blending, exactly even for the largest sizes. `Width`, `Height` and `At` read it, and every method is safe on a nil `*Bitmap`. A pixel with an alpha of 128 or more is drawn as an opaque color and a lighter one is left alone, so what is on the canvas shows through; black is a color, and a JPEG has no alpha, so all its pixels are drawn. Drawing clips, never reads or writes depth, visits only the pixels on the canvas and allocates nothing: 64 by 64 pixels take about 3.7 microseconds, scaled to 128 by 96 about 29, and one far off the canvas 0.3. Decoding a 64 by 64 PNG takes about 81 microseconds and allocates once for each pixel.
- `bitmap.Decode` refuses a file over 32 MiB, or one whose header says it has more than 4,194,304 pixels (2,048 by 2,048), before decoding it, with `ErrTooLarge`, and data that is not a JPEG or PNG with `ErrFormat`. `bitmap.DecodeLimit(r, maxBytes, maxPixels)` takes limits of your own, a limit that is not positive refuses every picture, and a source that never ends is read only up to the limit. The decoders are linked only into programs that import `bitmap`.
- A new package, `widget`, draws the pieces of a heads-up display and a menu in screen space. Each widget is a value with exported fields and a `Draw` method that takes a position and a size in whole cells, as `DrawBox` does. What runs off the frame is cut off, a size that is not positive draws nothing, and numbers as large as an `int` cannot wrap around onto the frame. Drawing allocates nothing. A style is an `engine.Cell` whose colors and attributes are used and whose character is not.
- `widget.Label` writes a line of text aligned `Left`, `Center` or `Right` in a width, counted in cells as `TextWidth` counts them and cut off where the width ends, with a combining mark kept with its character. Only the cells of the text are drawn.
- `widget.Panel` draws a border with a title on the top edge, cut to fit and shown from 7 cells wide, and fills the inside with `Fill`. A blank `Fill` with no background and no attributes, which the zero value is, is a hole that leaves the inside alone. A panel smaller than 2 by 2 draws nothing, as `DrawBox` does.
- `widget.Bar` draws a value from 0 to 1 as a run of cells filled from the left, rounded to the nearest cell. A smaller value is 0, a larger one is 1 and NaN is 0. `Full` and `Empty` are the cells, `█` and `░` where they are the zero `Cell`, and are drawn as they are.
- `widget.Menu` is a list of items with one selected. `Update(in)` moves the selection on Up and Down, wrapping around at the ends, and on Enter returns the index of the item and true; Escape and every other key are left to the game. `Draw` puts an item on each row, cut to the width, with `Reverse` toggled across the selected row, and scrolls a list longer than its rows to keep the selected item in the middle row. A `Selected` that is not an item is taken as 0, and `Draw` does not change the menu, which keeps no state of its own beyond `Selected`.
- `engine.TextWidth(text)` returns the number of cells `FrameBuffer.DrawText` fills with a string, not counting any it would clip: one for each character except combining marks and joiners, which have none. A wide or control character is one cell, drawn as `?`. It is for centering, right-aligning or cutting text where `DrawText` would put it.

### Changed

- Model textures find the kind of their picture and read its size from the header through the same internal code as `bitmap`. What a texture accepts and refuses, and the pixel budget, are as they were.
- `examples/world` has a coin panel at the top left, with the count and a bar that fills as coins are collected, and a pause menu: Esc now opens a menu to resume or quit, with the world standing still, instead of quitting at once.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-11

On top of 0.4.0 this release adds 2D collision, a tile map and a camera that follows the player. Nothing was removed, renamed or changed.

### Added

- A new package, `tilemap`: a grid of tiles for a game to be played on. `Tile` is an `engine.Cell` and whether it is `Solid`. `FromLines(legend, lines...)` makes a `Map` from text art, `New(w, h, fill)` from one tile, and `Width`, `Height`, `Bounds`, `At`, `Set` and `Solid` read and change it, safe outside the map and on a nil one. Outside the map is solid: the edge of the world is a wall. A map holds up to 4,194,304 cells and 65,535 kinds of tile, at two bytes a cell, and a bigger one is empty.
- `Map.Draw(fb, view)` draws the part of a map that is in a `geometry.Rect`, the camera, using only its top left corner, floored in the map. A tile that is a hole (blank, no background, no attributes) is not drawn and shows what is under it, so one map can be laid over another. 200 by 60 cells take about 20 microseconds, with no allocation.
- `Map.Move(box, delta)` moves a box along X and then along Y, stopping at solid tiles, so a box that goes into a wall at an angle slides along it. It looks at every tile along the way, so a long move cannot carry the box through a thin wall, and it stops with the box's edge exactly on the tile's edge, so there is no rounding gap and the box rests against a wall the same at any frame rate. It returns the moved box and whether it was stopped on each axis, takes 24 nanoseconds and allocates nothing, and its work is bounded by the size of the map whatever the delta. A box that starts inside a solid tile can move on, one outside the map cannot move, and an empty box or a NaN or infinite number is not moved.
- `Layer.DrawView(fb, view)` draws a sprite layer as seen through a `Rect`: each sprite goes to the cell its position is floored to, minus the cell the view's corner is floored to, both floored in the world first, so that sprites and tiles never slip against each other as the view moves. `Layer.Draw` is untouched and measures the same.
- `Rect.Center`, `Rect.CenterOn(p)` and `Rect.Within(bounds)` make a camera out of a rectangle: the screen's, centered on the player and kept inside the level (or centered on an axis where the level is smaller than the screen).
- `examples/world`: a level of 120 by 36 cells written as text art, a camera that follows the player, walls and water that stop and slide it, and spinning coins to collect. `make run EXAMPLE=./examples/world`.
- `geometry.Rect`, an axis-aligned rectangle of the points from `Min` up to but not including `Max`, half-open like the cells a sprite covers, so rectangles that only touch do not overlap. `NewRect`, `Width`, `Height`, `Size`, `Empty`, `Contains`, `Overlaps`, `Intersect` and `Translate`. A rectangle with no area, turned inside out or with a NaN in it is empty and overlaps nothing.
- `Rect.PushOutOf(o)`: the smallest move, along one axis, that takes a rectangle out of another, for walls and paddles. It picks the axis on which they overlap least and the way that is shorter (ties toward larger X, then Y, and X before Y), and is the zero vector when they do not overlap or when no finite move would do. It does not look at where the mover came from, so one that moves farther than its own size in a frame can come out the far side of a thin wall; after a push the boxes touch to within rounding, so round a pushed position (to a millionth, say) or it can be drawn a cell short.
- `Sprite.Bounds` is the box a sprite covers, from its `Position` as it is (not floored) by the size of the animation's current frame, else the frame, else 1 by 1, holes included; a sprite with nothing to draw, or at a NaN or infinite position, has an empty box. `Sprite.Overlaps` asks whether two boxes share area.
- `Layer.All` and `Layer.Hits(r)` range over the sprites of a layer, and over those whose boxes overlap a `Rect`, in draw order. They allocate nothing (1,000 sprites take about 4 microseconds to ask), the layer must not change while they run, and `Layer.Draw` is as fast as before.
- `examples/sprites` has a rock in the sea. The ship is pushed out of it, so steering into it stops the ship against its face and steering in at an angle slides it along; the ghost floats through. The ship's step is capped at 0.05 s so that a long frame cannot carry it through the rock.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-10

On top of 0.3.0 this release colors models: from the pictures their materials name, and from colors on their vertices. Nothing was removed or renamed.

### Added

- `Mesh.VertexColors`: a color for each vertex, drawn as the blend of a face's three corner colors at every cell, with the perspective-correct weights smooth shading uses for normals, flat or smooth, in cells (`DrawShaded`) and in pixels (`DrawShadedPixels`). A face cut by the near plane blends as the whole face would. A vertex color multiplies the color of a face that has one (`FaceColors`: a material's `Kd` or texture), so white leaves it as it is; a face without one is drawn in the vertex colors as they are. A corner with the zero `Color` takes what the face would be without vertex colors, and a default color there reads as black. A face none of whose corners has a color is drawn as before. A list of any other length is not used and `Validate` reports it; `Clone` copies it, `Fit` and `SmoothNormals` keep it, and wireframes ignore it.
- `LoadOBJ`, `LoadOBJLimit` and `LoadOBJFS` read the vertex colors of `v x y z r g b` lines: exactly six numbers, 0 to 1 and clamped as `Kd` is, black a color. Any other count, or numbers that are not understood, gives none and never fails the load. The list starts at the first colored vertex, so a file without colors has none, and a vertex copied for its normals keeps its color.
- `Color.RGB` returns a color's red, green and blue (a palette color as xterm defines it, the default color as black), so that colors can be mixed outside the engine.
- The viewer has a bundled octahedron with a color on each corner, between the icosphere and the torus. It opens in its own colors, and C puts them aside for the palette and back, as it does for the house.
- Texture colors on models. `LoadOBJFS` reads the `map_Kd` picture of a material (JPEG or PNG, found by its content, beside the OBJ) and the texture coordinates of the faces (`vt`), and colors each triangle with the nearest pixel of the picture at the middle of its three corners' coordinates, in `Mesh.FaceColors`: the picture repeats, `v` runs from the bottom, transparency is ignored and colors are not gamma converted, as for `Kd`. A triangle without usable coordinates, or whose picture could not be had, keeps the material's `Kd`, and a material with both shows the picture. One color for each triangle means a large one is flat; a model of small triangles looks textured. Only the plain `map_Kd name` form is read (not `-s`, `-o` and the like), and no other map.
- The picture is as untrusted as the model, and a problem with it never fails the load. Its size is read from the header and refused before decoding: 4096 by 4096 pixels (16,777,216) may be decoded per model in all, including a picture that then fails; 8 pictures are tried; a file is read up to 64 MiB or `maxBytes`. A picture is read when the first face that uses it comes, once. While loading, a decoded picture is held (up to 128 MiB, freed after) and 16 bytes for each `vt` line.

### Changed

- `mesh.Mesh` has a new field, `VertexColors`. Code that sets fields by name is unaffected, and a mesh without it draws as before. A `Mesh{...}` literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles, and `go vet` already warns about those: name the fields.
- `LoadOBJ` and `LoadOBJLimit` now read the colors of six-number vertex lines, which they used to ignore, so a model that carried colors loads with them. Reading such a line takes about 77 ns longer (a file of 60,000 colored vertices loads 27% slower); a file without colors loads in the same time and with the same allocations.
- `Mesh` is 24 bytes larger and is copied for every draw, so the shaded draw benchmarks take 1 to 4% longer (the largest on a scene of 200 small meshes; the wireframe benchmark, whose code did not change, moved 1 to 2% in the same runs). Drawing a mesh without vertex colors gives the same cells and allocates nothing, as before.
- A model that used to load with no colors, or some, may now load with all: the pirate ship test model went from 1,552 colored faces of 29,860 to all of them, and a model of 800,000 faces from none to all. Loading takes longer by the time to decode the picture (23 to 61 ms, and 615 to 844 ms for the two), and `LoadOBJFS` of a file with many `vt` lines and no texture takes about 17% longer to read them. `LoadOBJ` and `LoadOBJLimit` are unchanged and read no `vt`.
- Programs that import `mesh` now link the standard library's `image/jpeg` and `image/png` decoders, about 320 KB more in the viewer (3.08 to 3.40 MB); they also register those formats with `image.Decode` in the program, as importing them always does.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-09

On top of 0.2.0 this release adds sprites of several cells, see-through and animated, and wireframes drawn as half-block pixels. Nothing was removed or renamed.

### Added

- `Canvas.DrawLine` and `Canvas.DrawLineDepth` draw a line of pixels: the line `FrameBuffer.DrawLine` and `DrawLineDepth` draw, on twice as many rows, with the same perspective-correct depth (the formula is shared, so they cannot disagree). `DrawLine`, like `Set`, neither reads nor writes depth, and the zero Color erases.
- `Mesh.DrawPixels` and `Mesh.DrawDepthPixels` draw a wireframe into a `Canvas`, twice as fine from top to bottom as `Draw` and `DrawDepth`, with the same edges, near-plane cutting and skipping of what is off screen. `DrawPixels` ignores depth. `DrawDepthPixels` is hidden by nearer solids drawn into the same canvas and hides farther ones, whatever the order, to the pixel; call `Canvas.Compose` afterwards. A line is one pixel thick, and on a `Seamless` canvas a row of whole cells. `Draw` and `DrawDepth` are unchanged: every picture is identical, `Draw` is within 0.2% of its time and `DrawDepth` is 2 to 3% faster.
- cube3d draws its wireframe cube as pixel lines in half-block mode (M), hidden to the pixel by the cubes in front of it; the floor stays dotted. In the viewer, V draws the wireframe as pixel lines when half-blocks are on (M), in the palette color, and B (exact or solid edges) works with it; the HUD reads `wireframe, half-blocks, exact edges`.
- Sprites of several cells. `sprite.Frame` is a grid of cells: `FromLines(style, lines...)` makes one from text art (a space is a blank cell; the style's colors and attributes go on every rune that is not a space), `NewFrame(w, h)` makes a blank one, and `Width`, `Height`, `At`, `Set` and `Clone` work on it, all safe on a nil frame. `Sprite.Frame` draws it with its top left cell at `Position`, cut off cell by cell at every edge. A frame is limited to about a million cells; a bigger one comes back empty.
- `Sprite.Overlay` makes a sprite see-through: a blank cell with no background and no attributes is a hole where what is behind shows, and any other cell with no background of its own keeps the background underneath. It works for a single `Cell` too. Off by default, so existing sprites draw as before; on a cell written by `Canvas.Compose` only the background is kept. Plain single-cell sprites cost about 0.5 ns more each to draw (the larger `Sprite` and the extra test): 1,000 of them take 2.0 microseconds where they took 1.4, with no allocation.
- `sprite.Animation`: frames at a speed, advanced from `Update` with `Advance(dt)`, so it plays at the same speed at any frame rate. `NewAnimation(fps, frames...)`, `Loop`, `Index`, `Frame`, `Done` and `Reset`; it plays once unless `Loop` is set, a time that is negative or not a number is ignored, and an `FPS` that is not positive and finite does not run. `Sprite.Animation` takes the place of `Sprite.Frame`, which takes the place of `Sprite.Cell`.
- `examples/sprites`: a ship drawn from text art that the arrow keys steer, a figure with a four-frame walk cycle that comes back in from the left, and a ghost that blinks and floats over the sea, all see-through. `make run EXAMPLE=./examples/sprites`.

### Changed

- `sprite.Sprite` has three new fields: `Frame`, `Animation` and `Overlay`. Code that sets fields by name is unaffected, and a sprite with none of them set draws as before. A `Sprite{...}` literal that lists its values without field names no longer compiles, and `go vet` already warns about those: name the fields.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-08

The module path is now `glonk.dev`, which is a breaking change: see Changed, and "Moved to glonk.dev" in the README for the commands that move a project. On top of 0.1.0 this release adds smooth shading, true color, depth-tested wireframes and colors on models, and sends the terminal far less per frame.

### Added

- Smooth shading. `Mesh.Normals`, one normal per vertex in model space, makes `DrawShaded` and `DrawShadedPixels` light every cell instead of every face, from the position and normal blended across the face with perspective-correct weights; a mesh without `Normals` is lit as before. A `Light` is then asked about the place on the surface and the unit normal there. A zero normal means none here and takes the face's own; normals turn with the `Transform` and ignore its scale; a list of the wrong length or with a number that is not finite is not used (the mesh is shaded flat) and `Validate` reports it. A back face of a `DoubleSided` mesh uses the flipped normal, and near-plane clipping blends the position and normal at the cut. Smooth shading costs about 12 to 23% more than facets on a scene of spheres; flat meshes pay nothing.
- `Mesh.SmoothNormals` returns a copy with normals found by averaging the faces that share each vertex, weighted by the angle each makes there, so cutting a surface differently does not change how it is lit. `Clone` and `Fit` carry `Normals`.
- `Sphere`, `Cylinder`, `Cone` and `Plane`: solid meshes with normals, hard edges where a surface has one (the caps of a cylinder, the base of a cone), and wireframe edges that follow the form. Segments are brought into 3 to 512 and rings into 2 to 512, a size that is negative or not finite counts as 0, and the result always passes `Validate`.
- `Point`, a light from one place that fades with distance: `Intensity` (0 means 1) times how squarely the surface faces it times `(1 - distance/Range)` squared. `Range` 0 means no fading; a negative `Range` gives no light.
- `LoadOBJ` reads `vn` and the normal index of faces (`v//vn`, `v/vt/vn`, negative indices). A place that a file lights with several normals is split into one vertex for each, so a cube comes back with 24 vertices; edges still join the original places. A normal index that is missing, not a number or names no normal read so far leaves the corner without one instead of failing the load, as texture indices always have been ignored. A `vn` statement that is malformed is an error naming the line.
- `FrameBuffer.FillTriangleFunc` and `Canvas.FillTriangleFunc` fill a triangle by asking a function for each covered cell, with the perspective-correct weights of the three corners there. The depth test runs first, so the function is never asked about a hidden cell.
- The viewer shows a sphere, a cylinder and a cone after the OBJ samples. F switches the current model between smooth and faceted (a model without normals is smoothed from its faces), and L switches the light between the sun and a lamp that follows the camera. The HUD names the shading and the light.
- `Mesh.DrawDepth`, a wireframe with depth: each cell of each edge is stored with its distance, so a nearer surface hides it, it hides a farther one, and the order of drawing does not matter. `Draw` is unchanged and still paints over everything. Strictly nearer wins and a tie keeps what is there, so a line lying exactly on a surface flickers; there is no bias. Solids drawn into a `Canvas` hide it once `Compose` has run.
- `FrameBuffer.DrawLineDepth` draws the cells `DrawLine` does, each stored with `SetDepth`, with a perspective-correct depth between the two ends (1/z runs evenly along the line); a depth that is not positive draws nothing.
- cube3d has a seventh cube, a cyan wireframe drawn with `DrawDepth`: the solid cubes hide the part of its edges they cover, in character and half-block modes alike.
- `Mesh.FaceColors`: a color for each face, or none, that replaces `Material.Color` for that face in `DrawShaded` and `DrawShadedPixels`, flat or smooth, with the brightness still shown by the character or the shade. A zero entry takes `Material.Color`; a list of any other length is not used and `Validate` reports it; `Clone`, `Fit` and `SmoothNormals` keep it. A mesh without it draws as before, with no allocation and within 1% of the time on the benchmark scenes.
- `LoadOBJFS(fsys, name, maxBytes)` loads an OBJ from an `fs.FS` with the colors of its materials: `mtllib` names MTL files beside it, and `usemtl` picks the `Kd` for the faces after it (a polygon gives all its triangles the color). Only `Kd` is read, with no gamma conversion. A name that is not a plain path inside the OBJ's folder, a file that is missing, broken or over 1 MiB (or `maxBytes`), and an unknown material never fail the load: the faces come out with no color. At most 8 files and 10,000 materials are read per OBJ. `LoadOBJ` and `LoadOBJLimit` still skip materials. Loading a file with no materials takes the same time and memory as before.
- The viewer's house has a `house.mtl` and opens in its own colors. C goes from them through the six palette colors and back; a model without colors steps the palette as before. A file given on the command line is colored by the `.mtl` beside it. The HUD says `model colors` while they show.
- True color. An `RGB` color is sent exactly (`ESC[38;2;r;g;b m`) when `COLORTERM` is `truecolor` or `24bit` or `TERM` names true color, and as the nearest palette color otherwise, as before. `Config.Colors` (`ColorsAuto`, `Colors256`, `ColorsTrue`) overrides the detection, for tmux and ssh where `COLORTERM` often does not arrive. `Palette` colors are never sent as RGB, so the user's theme still applies to them. A headless `Run` uses 256 colors unless `ColorsTrue` is set, so what it writes never depends on the environment.

### Changed

- **Breaking:** the module path is now `glonk.dev`, where up to 0.1.0 it was `github.com/kyrokohan/glonk`. Imports become `glonk.dev/engine`, `glonk.dev/mesh`, `glonk.dev/geometry` and `glonk.dev/sprite`. The code is still hosted on GitHub; `glonk.dev` tells the go command where. Nothing else in the API changed because of this.
- The viewer reads OBJ files up to 128 MiB, where it stopped at 64, so a model of 800,000 triangles (81 MB) loads.
- A model file whose faces refer to normals now comes back with `Normals`, and so is drawn smooth where it used to be drawn flat. A file whose faces use no normals loads as it did, with no `Normals`, in the same time and with a little less memory (93 MB for the 3.7 MB benchmark file, 20% fewer allocations). Memory use is roughly 25 times the input size, up to about 35 for a file that gives most corners a normal of their own; `LoadOBJLimit` now says 35.
- Drawing a mesh allocates nothing once warm, where it took 7 to 40 allocations a draw, and is up to about 20% faster on the benchmark scene. The camera's trigonometry is worked out once per draw instead of once per vertex, with the same arithmetic in the same order, so no picture moved.
- `Canvas.Compose` also records depth: each cell it fills gets the depth of its nearer pixel in the `FrameBuffer`, in place of the depth it had. Anything drawn into the cells afterwards with a depth (`FillTriangle`, `DrawLineDepth`, `SetDepth`) is now hidden behind pixels that are nearer; before, it always landed on top. Drawing without a depth (`Set`, `DrawLine`, `DrawText`) is unaffected, and the cells Compose writes are the same. A pixel set with `Set` has no depth and counts as infinitely far. `Compose` takes about 10 microseconds longer on a full 200x60 canvas.
- Drawing a mesh starts by asking whether any of it can be on screen, and skips it if not, before it is placed, transformed or lit. No picture changes. A scene of a thousand cubes with 4% in view draws 58 to 75% faster, and a large mesh behind the camera 80 to 95% faster; scenes with everything in view are within noise.
- `Shade` returns an exact RGB color for every kind of color (a palette color is read as xterm defines it, the default color as black), so shades are finer on a true color terminal. On a terminal with 256 colors the shades look as before; about 2% of them land on the neighboring palette color, always one as near to the true shade. Code that compared `Shade(Palette(n), x)` with a palette color needs to change.

- `Run` sends a terminal only the cells that changed since the last frame, as one synchronized update (terminal mode 2026, which terminals that do not know it ignore), instead of every cell of every frame. A screen that does not change writes nothing, and the example programs write 74% to 100% fewer bytes. The screen is drawn again in full after a resize, after Ctrl-Z, after a failed write and when the terminal reports the window regained focus. A headless `Run` still writes every frame whole. Text that something else prints to the terminal during a run is no longer overwritten by the next frame.
- Escape sequences are built without `fmt` into a buffer that is reused from frame to frame, and a change of colors or attributes is written as one sequence. A frame takes 2 to 7 times less time to produce, allocates nothing once running, and a color-heavy frame is up to 9% smaller.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-08

The first release. The API is pre-1.0 and may still change. The README lists the known limits of what exists today.

### Added

**engine**
- `Run` drives a `Game` at a fixed frame rate (1 to 1000) and owns the terminal: alternate screen, raw mode, restored on return, on errors and on panics in game code. The frame fills the terminal and follows resizes, or has a fixed size and shows the part that fits.
- `FrameBuffer` of `Cell`s with a `Foreground` and `Background` `Color` and text `Attrs` (`Bold`, `Dim`, `Underline`, `Reverse`); `DrawLine`, `DrawBox`, `DrawText`, and `FillTriangle` with a depth buffer.
- `Color` is a type: the terminal default (the zero value), `Palette(n)` for the 256-color palette, or `RGB(r, g, b)`. RGB is shown as the nearest palette color until true color output is added.
- `Canvas`: a pixel layer with two pixels per cell, composed into half-block characters, with a `Seamless` mode for terminals that draw seams. `Shade` maps a color to a brighter or darker one.
- Keyboard `Input` with held keys and one-shot presses. Terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol report real key releases, so several keys can be held at once; everywhere else held keys fall back to timing. Escape sequences split across reads are reassembled, a lone Escape counts after 50 ms, Alt chords are ignored, and keys typed while glonk starts up are kept.
- `Config.Input` and `Config.Output` (default `os.Stdin` and `os.Stdout`) for running on another terminal such as `/dev/tty`, or on none: with an input or output that is not a terminal, `Run` runs headless at the given `Width` and `Height`, writes only the frames, and leaves terminal settings and signals alone, so a game can be tested with key bytes in and a buffer out. With neither a terminal nor a size, `Run` returns an error that says so.
- `Resizer`: a game with a `Resize(width, height int)` method is told the frame size before the first `Update` and whenever it changes.
- A written contract for how `Run` ends and what it calls: it returns `nil` when the player quits (Ctrl-C, `ErrQuit`, a quit signal) and the context's error (`context.Canceled`, `context.DeadlineExceeded`) when the context ends it, at once if the context is already done; `Resize`, `Update` and `Draw` are called one at a time from one goroutine, none starts once the context is done, and none is called after `Run` returns.
- Test helpers: `Keyboard` presses and releases keys for a game's `Update` (exact, like a terminal that reports releases, with no clock), and `FrameBuffer` has a `String` method, so a whole screen can be compared as text and `fmt.Println(fb)` prints it.
- Terminal safety: `SIGINT`, `SIGTERM` and `SIGHUP` end `Run` cleanly (a second signal kills a stuck game); Ctrl-Z suspends and `fg` resumes; `Run` leaves stdin alone once it returns; line wrapping is off while a program runs.
- Output safety: every cell is checked before it is sent, so control characters and escape sequences become `?`, wide characters become `?`, combining marks are skipped by `DrawText`, and NUL is a space.

**mesh**
- `Mesh` with edges and triangular faces; `Cube`, `Grid`, `LoadOBJ` and `LoadOBJLimit` (which bounds untrusted input); `Fit`, `Bounds`, `Validate` and `Clone`.
- `Transform` places a mesh: `Position`, `Yaw`, `Pitch`, `Roll` and a uniform `Scale` (0 means 1), in `Mesh.Transform`. Yaw and Pitch turn a model the way they turn a `Camera`: positive yaw swings the front toward +x, positive pitch tips it up. `Transform.Apply` says where a model point ends up. A transform with a number that is not finite, or a negative scale, draws nothing and `Validate` reports it.
- `Material` says how a mesh looks shaded: a `Color` and a `Ramp` of brightness characters (default `.:-=+*#%@`), in `Mesh.Material`; the draw calls no longer take a color.
- `Light` is an interface with `Directional` and `Ambient` lights, and your own; `DrawShaded` and `DrawShadedPixels` take any number. Lights add up without passing full brightness, so one ambient light and one directional light give what the single combined light gave before.
- Perspective `Camera` with yaw, pitch, field of view, a near plane and a `CellAspect` (how much taller than wide a cell is; fields left at zero use defaults), and `Project`, `Forward`, `Right` and `Look` for putting things on screen and moving around; wireframe `Draw`; `DrawShaded` and `DrawShadedPixels` with depth testing, back-face culling and near-plane clipping; `DoubleSided` for models wound the other way.
- Drawing does not panic on bad meshes, transforms or cameras: edges and faces that name a missing vertex are skipped, and a camera with nonsense values sees nothing.

**sprite**, **geometry**
- `Sprite` is an `engine.Cell` (character, colors, attributes) at a `geometry.Vector2` position, drawn in a `Layer` that can `Add`, `Remove`, `Clear` and count (`Len`) its sprites; a sprite replaces the whole cell under it. `Vector2` and `Vector3`.

**Everything else**
- Three example programs (`bounce2d`, `cube3d` and a model `viewer`), runnable examples in the package documentation, fuzz tests for the parsers, rasterizers and clipping, and a test that keeps the documentation complete.
- Continuous integration (format, vet and race tests on Linux and macOS for Go 1.26 and 1.27, cross-compilation, 32-bit tests), Dependabot, a security policy and contributing notes.
